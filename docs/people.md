# People

Every person in the game is the same character: the garage crew, the race crowds, the street's walkers and idlers and every driver. One look, one mesh, one shader rig and one wardrobe (`render/people/looks.ts`), so they read as the same world and stay cheap enough to draw by the hundred.

## The look

Stylized, with a light touch of realism where the camera gets close. Rounded low-poly shapes with strong silhouettes: an egg of a head with a chin, a lathed torso that tapers to the waist and is a little flatter behind than at the chest, capsule limbs with a calf and a bicep, mitten hands, rounded shoes on soles. At hero and mid the head gets a face: small dark flattened eyes (glossy, so they catch a glint), brows in the hair's color, a nose and ears, and the back of the skull lifts so the neck shows; hero adds a thumb. The shoulders sit wide and high on the torso so the arms grow out of them rather than hang off a ball. It stays the same toy-like character and never reaches for detail that would turn uncanny; the crowd and far levels are faceless, since a face there is a few pixels. Clothes are flat colors, no textures. At night most people are seen as shapes under street light, which is where this style holds and realism falls apart first, so character comes from silhouette and color.

Per person options (`WEAR` bits, all 24 of the float's exact bits are in use). Two groups are small numbers rather than bits, so options that exclude each other share them:

- **Head** (cap, beanie and hood bits: `HEAD_STATE`): bare, a cap, a cap worn backwards, a beanie, a bucket hat, a bandana, a hood, headphones. The cap's crown serves the cap both ways and the bandana; the beanie's serves the bucket hat.
- **Hair** (hair and long bits: `HAIR_STATE`): shaved or close cropped, swept, long (a fall down the back that shows under any hat but the hood) or big curls (bare heads only). Swept hair hides under a hat, so looks set hair and hat independently.
- **Clothes**: an open jacket (a shell over the torso with lapels near, in the sleeves' color, which then colors the upper arms too), cargo pockets, baggy legs that flare over the shoe, a backpack, a name patch, a hi-vis stripe, gloves; and the shop's tool belt, coverall sleeves tied at the waist, work boots and a rag in the back pocket (hero only, since only the crew wears them).
- **Bare skin by color, not shape**: a tank top (upper arms and shoulders), shorts (shins) and a crop top (a midriff band, a part of the torso's lathe) are parts the shader colors with the skin when the bit is set. Bare forearms need no bit: a look sets `sleeves` to its skin tone.
- **In the right hand**: a phone, a flag, a glowstick, or at hero a cup (red, standing up when the arm hangs). A gold chain is hero only.

Seven colors per person: skin, shirt, sleeves (the forearms, the hood, the jacket, tied coverall sleeves), trousers, shoes, hair, cap (whatever is on the head, the backpack, the headphones' cans). Fixed colors for the rest: the patch's cream, the chain's gold, the belt's leather, the shop's red rag and cup, the eyes, a sneaker's light midsole or a boot's dark one. The crew's mugs, cans, wrenches and boxes are separate instanced meshes placed from `personFrame()`.

Shading, all free of per frame cost: occlusion baked into the vertices where the body folds (armpits, crotch, under the chin, under the brim, behind the backpack), a roughness per part (matte cloth, skin a touch less, glossier shoes and plastics, the stripe's reflective tape, a polished chain and wet eyes) and a faint cool rim in the fragment so figures separate from the night.

### Three wardrobes

Every list is cut from one palette (`C` in `looks.ts`), so every context is a subset of the same world, and every look draws a fixed number of rolls whatever it picks. The three contexts are there so a glance tells a race-goer from a passer-by from a mechanic.

- **`raceLook`**, the race crowds (and the impostor sheet): young, counter-culture, action sports and streetwear, with the first Fast and the Furious (2001) as the reference. Tight and oversized tees, tank tops, crop tops, jerseys in team colors, hoodies, open bombers and track jackets; baggy cargos, wide-leg jeans, shorts, track pants; caps worn backwards, bucket hats, bandanas, headphones; bleached and dyed hair on about a fifth; loud sneakers, chains, backpacks. Dark bases with neon and racing-team colors on top, mostly bare arms on a warm night. What they hold (phone, flag, glowstick) is the stands' to add. The drivers dress from it too (a cap and gloves put on, nothing in the hands or on the back).
- **`streetLook`**, pedestrians (walkers and idlers through `idleLook`, with the seed's warm or cool tint): everyday and muted. Jackets and coats over a tee, sweaters, hoodies, tees; jeans, chinos, dark trousers; the odd bright piece, a beanie, a cap or the hood up, a bag on a few.
- **`shopLook`**, the garage crew: an F&F shop floor in dark, oil-stained blues, greys and reds (a grime roll drifts each outfit toward oil). Coveralls rolled down and tied at the waist over a tank (Vince), a work shirt with a name patch and rolled sleeves, a tank with a tool belt and black cargos (Dom), full coveralls, a hoodie, a tee; work boots on most, backwards caps, beanies and bandanas, gloves and a rag on some, the hi-vis stripe rare. `shopLook(rnd, kind)` asks for an outfit by name; the crew (`garage/showroomCrew.ts`, `CREW`) gives each of its seven a character that way.

## Impostors

Past `CROWD_IMPOSTOR.distance` (`race/crowds.ts`: Low 40 m, Medium 60 m, High 90 m) a spectator is a camera-facing quad from a sprite sheet, so a stand of 300 costs 600 triangles instead of 60k (the far level) or 250k (the crowd level). `people/impostors.ts` bakes the sheet once per page from the live rig: the far level, drawn by the crowd's own pose shader into a render target by one instanced draw, orthographic, one cell per look x pose x frame x angle. Looks are a farthest point pick from the race wardrobe (skin tone x the colors the far level shows x its outline changes: a hat, a bucket brim, long hair, a backpack; with a phone, a flag or a glowstick in hand for the holders), poses are idle, clapping, arms up and jumping at the excitement the rig would show, frames step through the pose's cycle, angles are the person's yaw against the camera (4 at Low and Medium, 8 at High). Sheets: Low 512 px (1.4 MB with mips), Medium 1024 px (5.6 MB), High 2048 px (22 MB), cached per renderer. Each instance picks its cell in the vertex shader from its excitement, the clock and the camera direction, and its look from the nearest baked one (chosen once on the CPU at build). The sheet holds albedo (color times the baked occlusion) and a glow mask in alpha, not light: the material is the standard material with a normal facing the camera and tilted to the sky, so the hemisphere, moon, lamp pools and both fogs light it as they light a person, a lit phone screen is emission that fades with distance as the rig's emitters do, and the rim is a constant. The switch is a per person dither: the far level discards a pixel where the fade (0 to 1 over distance +- `fade` / 2) is above one shared screen-door noise, the impostor where it is at or below, so each pixel belongs to exactly one of them and nothing pops or blends. Batches skip the level none of their people are in range of. With no renderer (Vitest, `perf:scene`) or a failed bake there is no sheet and the stands keep the far level out to the draw distance, so the headless triangle counts are the rig's and do not show the saving.

## The rig

15 bones in a fixed chain: hips, spine, head; shoulder, elbow, hand per arm; hip, knee, ankle per leg (the foot is kept flat). A pose (`PersonPose`) is 16 numbers: hip height, spine lean/tilt/twist, head turn/pitch, per arm raise/out/elbow, per leg thigh swing/knee bend. `plantFeet()` solves the legs to stand feet on the floor. The vertex shader (`RIG_GLSL`) and `personFrame()` are the same chain and must change together.

## In the city

- **Drivers** are in (`render/people/drivers.ts`): one `People` at the mid level for the whole field, seated from each car's measured cabin (`SEATS`: cushion, seat back and wheel stations from the shipped GLB's `Interior` faces; measure a new car before it ships), each arm solved (two bones) to a grip on a wheel placed where a seated driver's hands fall, the grips going round the rim with the steer angle (a car whose donor wheel survived, the Coupe, names its own), head into the turn, body against the roll, through the tinted glass. The menu's cruising car has one too.
- **The plan** (`city/people/people.ts`, `peopleOf(world)`): every person of the city is one `WorldPerson` (a gate stand, an idler or a walker) in one list, planned once per World at one canonical density (`CANON`) whatever the graphics quality. The sim collides with them, so every tier must draw them all; quality only sets draw and level-of-detail distances (`IDLE_QUALITY`, `WALKERS` in `walkers.ts`, `CROWD_QUALITY.lodNear`), never how many there are. Meet crowds and market pens stay render only (behind rails the cars cannot reach), quality scaled, with no dodge and no hits; `meetPlan` keeps them clear of the plan's people.
- **Idlers** (`render/people/idlers.ts`, spots from `city/people/idleSpots.ts`) stand, sit and lean at bus stops, benches, cafe tables, park lamps and building fronts (pairs under the frontage of the busier streets: the shop bays are decided render side, so the plan cannot read them); they turn their heads to the nearest of four cars and flinch (pose flavour), and step out of the way and fall like everyone (below). The style cuts into a band, so a stander has their arms crossed, hands in pockets, weight on one leg with a hip pop, a watch checked every ~14 s, or (plain) a stretch every ~40 s; a sitter crosses their legs or rests their elbows on their knees; a leaner puts one foot up on the wall; a cup holder sips every ~9 s; headphones bob to a 128 bpm beat. Pairs and groups the plan puts together (a bench's sitters, a frontage pair, a plaza circle) share a phase and take turns gesturing and turning to each other on a 7 s cycle (`gestures.ts`, `talkCodes`). A destroyed prop knocks its sitters down in the sim, so they ragdoll like anyone else.
- **Walkers** (`render/people/walkers.ts`, paths and the roster from `city/people/walkPaths.ts`) walk the sidewalks with no per frame CPU at all, Low included (far rigs only there, a short draw distance: they are sim bodies at every tier). At build, each ordinary street's sidewalk, each side, is sampled along its middle and a small dynamic program picks the lane (0.25 m steps) that clears every prop by its collision radius, the walls, the curb and any road cut (a bus bay), so a detour is a smooth bend; the best lane through each clear run is simplified to at most five points and cut into paths. A cell (100 m) gets walkers in proportion to its path length, boosted downtown and toward the route. A walker is a stretch of one path (often running to a corner), a speed (1.1-1.6 m/s), a phase and a look; its instance matrix is the path's first point and four `vec4` attributes hold the rest (x, y, z from the start and the length walked, padded by repeating the last), so one draw per level of detail per cell. The vertex shader turns the people clock and the phase into the cycle (one leg, a pause to turn round and then, by style, glance about, check a watch or stretch, the leg back, a pause), picks the point and a corner smoothed heading from the polyline and swings the legs and arms from the *distance walked* (a stride is about 1.35 m), so feet do not skate and position is a pure function of the clock. `walkerAt` (`walkPaths.ts`) is its TypeScript twin, which the sim collides with: change them together. In a race the clock is the sim's interpolated time, so the drawn walker is where the sim has it; the menu city runs its own. They keep to the right of their way. Crowd detail within `near` m of a cell, far detail beyond. `PersonShader.root` is how a variant places the person (a `mat4` its pose snippet sets) after the rig. Walkers use all 15 usable vertex attributes (software WebGL counts the unused `uv` against the 16): the wall behind is an angle in `iData.w`, `prefer` and the room behind share `iHit.w`, the segment count is derived from the padded lengths.
- **Race crowds** are on the character: `race/crowds.ts` keeps its stands, excitement and cheer waves, and its shader computes the pose per instance from excitement, a phase and a style (`CROWD_POSE`: idle sway and weight shifting, a glance around or by style band arms crossed, hands in pockets or on hips, weight on one leg, a dance bob (headphones always bob); talking pairs taking turns; the head following the nearest car; clapping as it rises, or pointing at the road for a share of the people; arms up waving and jumping at the top; a phone held up in one hand or two, higher as it rises, or a flag waved). The front row of a meet or market pen leans on its rail, forearms resting. **Group phases**: a person's phase is their group's (`WorldPerson.groupPhase`: friends in a run along a stand, a prop's people, a frontage pair, a plaza circle; the people of one 2.2 m cell in a render-only section) plus a small jitter, so a group claps, cheers, bobs and takes turns talking together while groups stay out of step with each other and the stands cheer in clusters. The talking code (voice 1 or 2, the side of the nearest groupmate) and the rail bit ride in `iData.z`. The impostor sheet bakes the plain idle (no band, no group), so sprites do not show the variants. The start / finish stands are the plan's gate people (`gateStands.ts`), dressed with `raceLook`; the meets' and market pens' people are render only. A material variant passes that GLSL to `personMaterial()` (`PersonShader`), which is how any new context gets its own motion without touching the rig.
- **Dodging and falling** (`render/people/motion.ts`): `PEOPLE_SIDESTEP_GLSL` is the GLSL twin of the sim's `sidestep()` (`src/sim/people.ts`; its JSDoc is the spec, the numbers are baked from `PEOPLE` and `drivingParams.json`'s `people` section): each person steps out of a car's path by the offset the sim moves them by, with a stepping pose (`pmStepPose`), from the cars nearest their batch (`PEOPLE_MAX_CARS` = 4, one `uCars` array and material per batch or cell: three uploads a material's uniforms only when the material changes between draws, so a shared uniform written per mesh would not reach consecutive batches). `iHit` is (hit time on the people clock, fling velocity x and z, seed); `PeopleHits` copies the sim's hits (`RaceState.people`) into it only when `state.hits` changes, one integer compare a frame otherwise. A hit person is the ragdoll (`pmRagdoll`, stateless from the clock minus the hit time): thrown at the car's share of its speed, airborne for 2 vy0 / g, a slide braking at 9 m/s^2, a spin that dies with it, the body pitching to flat, limbs flailing and settling into a sprawl; the fall starts where the sim registered the hit (`PeopleState.hitX/hitZ`: the person at their dodged place for a car, their seat for a sitter a broken prop took): `PeopleHits` moves the instance's matrix translation there once at the hit (a walker's by what its frozen walk adds, `walkerAt`) and back when they stand up again, so the body does not jump. Hit people stay on the far level (the sprites discard them) and their batch's bounds grow for the flung. Walkers freeze at the hit time and fall from there.
- **The people clock**: in a race the stands, idlers and walkers run on `max(0, tick - 1 + alpha) / 60` s (the interpolated sim time, as the districts do), not the scene clock, so a pause freezes them with the sim and the dodge, the walkers and the falls agree with what the sim tested; the menu city runs its own clock with no hits.
- **Collisions** are the sim's (`src/sim/people.ts`): each person is a circle at their place at the sim clock plus the stateless dodge, tested against each car's box like a prop; slow contact holds like a short wall, faster the car loses speed by momentum exchange and the person goes down for the race (`PeopleState`, a `'person'` event). Nothing in `render/` writes any of it.
