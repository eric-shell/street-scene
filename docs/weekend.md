# Weekend specials

Saturdays and Sundays are the busiest days, so every weekend's daily seed carries one **weekend special**: a large drivable set piece carved into the city the seed already makes, 6 to 12 blocks of it, that the circuit and the sprint must drive through from an entry to an exit. There are three, one per weekend day in rotation:

| Kind | Name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `railYard` | The Rail Yard | A diesel classification yard round its throat road: receiving tracks, a hump into the bowl, an overbridge over the whole yard, the dock and the transfer pad, a banked turnaround road. |
| `underground` | The Underground | A half-built cut-and-cover expressway: the spine sunk in a trench, a third of it roofed by a works plaza, service roads, Bailey bridges, a utility gallery, kickers up between the levels. |
| `scrapyard` | The Scrapyard | Five ways through a yard of scrap mountains: the concrete haul road, a banked figure eight, a canyon of stacked wrecks with the crusher's press and kicker, a deck of bales four metres up, and the perimeter road. |

Code: `src/city/weekend.ts` (the pick), `src/city/districts/weekendRegion.ts` (the region framework: the carve, the spine, the mandate, progress, the shared build pieces), `src/city/districts/<kind>.ts` and `src/render/districts/<kind>.ts` (each area's planner and renderer), `scripts/weekendRate.ts` (the mandate's rates over the coming weekends). The menu's event card shows a "Weekend special" chip and the special's tagline when `world.event.special` is set.

## How the kind is chosen

The daily seed is the Pacific date as `YYYYMMDD` (`src/seed.ts`), and `seedFromString` returns a 1-9 digit string as that number. `weekendOf(seed)` reads the date back out of the digits with a days-from-civil formula (no `Date`): it is null unless the seed is exactly eight digits, a valid calendar date (leap years right), in 2025-2099, on a Saturday or Sunday. The sim never reads the clock, so a Saturday link opened on a Tuesday rebuilds the Saturday city and a replay (which carries only the seed) rebuilds the world it was recorded in. A random 8-digit menu seed reads as a weekend date about 0.03% of the time and gets a special too; that is accepted.

`ordinal` counts weekend days since Saturday 2026-01-03 (Saturday 0, its Sunday 1, the next Saturday 2, ...; negative before it), and `kind = WEEKEND_FEATURES[ordinal mod 3]`. A Saturday and its Sunday therefore differ, and the cycle takes a day and a half.

Weekday seeds are untouched bit for bit: the weekend modules return at once unless `ctx.weekend?.kind` is theirs, and each module draws only from its own stream.

## The slot in the registry

`DISTRICT_MODULES` (`src/city/districts/index.ts`) runs `railYardModule`, `undergroundModule`, `scrapyardModule` right after `backAlleyModule` and before `marketModule`. Their `carve` runs before anything plans (before the lake's carve, so the lake keeps off a region's claims); their `plan` runs after the topology hook, so it sees both routes as `ctx.event.routes`. `PlanContext.weekend` carries the pick for every module; `City.weekend` / `World.weekend` expose it (null on a weekday). The night market's width-only `graphChanged` after the weekend slot tolerates a weekend area's face claims (`CORRIDOR_AREAS`).

`CityEvent.special` (`{ kind, name, tagline }`) is filled by `resolveEvent` only when the seed is a weekend date, the region was planned and both routes drive its spine, so the menu never promises a special the race does not go through.

## The region (`districts/weekendRegion.ts`)

Every special is built in a **region**: a rectangle of 6 to 12 grid cells carved out of the city, its inner streets deleted, with a **spine** of `feature` edges running from an **entry** junction on its ring to an **exit** junction on the opposite side. The circuit and the sprint must drive the spine end to end, so the race goes through the set piece, the way it goes down the channel's floor: progress, the AI's line, the lost check and the route's closures follow it for free. The module's own ways through (its **lines**) are open ground, decks and trenches around the spine inside the region's polygon.

**The pick** (`carveRegion`, the module's `carve`, lifecycle step 0: deleting streets renumbers every id, as the lake's carve does). Each module declares its shapes as `WeekendShape[]` (`along` the spine x `across` it; each tried with the spine along the rows and along the columns): the Rail Yard and the Underground 2 across x 4 to 6 along on a straight spine, the Scrapyard 3 x 3, 4 x 3 and 3 x 4 on a sweeping one. A rectangle must be one cell in from the map edge (`CFG.edgeCells`), every ring junction unclaimed and no roundabout, every ring side a plain street (up to `maxRestore` sides the grid generator dropped are laid again as straight streets, within the degree cap), its inner rows off the expressway's first rows (the ring's rows may carry it), inside the special-area budget (its footprint is counted at carve), and kept clear by the channel: the channel's plan is run on the edited copy (`lake.ts channelPlanOn`) and its band must keep off the polygon and leave every ring side and spine piece untouched. Downtown's core is tried last. Candidates are sorted by the spine's bend (and downtown) with a small draw from the module's stream breaking near ties; the first that passes every check on a copy of the graph is carved for real.

**The ends.** The entry and the exit are side junctions (no corners) of the two sides across the spine's axis where a plain street arrives straight out from the next grid junction, at least `minApproach` m, within `maxApproachDeg` of square. The spine's first piece carries straight on along that street for `flare` m (38) at the street's width: the flare, never a 90 degree gate; the last piece flares back to the exit street. A straight spine (`straight: true`) takes ends whose approaches are within `maxStraightDeg` of the chord and lays every node on it; a sweeping spine is a cubic Bezier off both approaches with the nodes evenly spaced along it (no node turns more than 32 degrees, every point `spineWidth / 2 + 8` m inside the ring).

**The spine.** The rectangle's inner junctions become the spine's bend nodes (moved onto it, `bend` set, kept at their grid ids so `gridNode` still answers), so a spine has `(across - 1) x (along - 1) + 2` nodes. Its edges are `RoadKind` **`feature`** (`roadGraph.ts`, `isFeatureEdge`): sized by the module (`addEdge(g, a, b, 'feature', { width, lanes: 2, sidewalk: 0 })`; `roadDims('feature')` returns `CITY.districts.weekend.spineWidth` and never throws), drawn as asphalt by `cityMeshes` with no lane paint, curbs or sidewalks; no frontage, buildings, furniture, trees, parking, bus bays, crosswalks, decals, people paths, crown or humps beside or on them; no traffic kind drives them; route closures never close one and no corner rail stands at a node on one; the start grid, the circuit's start, the sprint's start and finish and the waypoint race's ends keep off them, and the drag strip's kinds leave them out. `segWidth` takes the edge's width.

**Claims.** The area claims the spine, the ring's junctions and pieces, both approach streets and their first nodes at carve (the El, the expressway, the bluff, roundabouts, alleys, shortcuts, the market and the lake keep off them), and in `plan` (`planRegion`, its registry slot right after the topology hook) every block face bounded only by its own edges, the way the lake claims its merged block.

**What the area stores** (ids in params are not renumbered, so they live in the lists, which `districts/index.ts` carries through every later renumbering in order): `nodes` = the spine entry to exit (`params.spineNodes`), the two approach streets' first nodes (entry side, exit side), the ring's other junctions, then the module's line nodes; `edges` = the spine's pieces (`spineNodes - 1`), the module's feature edges (`params.lineEdges`), the ring's pieces, both approach streets' pieces (`params.approachEdges`). `polygon` is the merged block's ring (the ring streets' outer sidewalk line), `path` the spine's points entry to exit. `regionOf(area)` decodes all of it (`Region`); `weekendRegion(ctx)` finds the seed's. Params also carry `along`, `across`, `i0`, `j0`, `cellsI`, `cellsJ`, `spineWidth`, `widthE`, `widthX`, `straight`, `seed` (the area's own stream seed: derive per use, `deriveSeed(params.seed, '<kind>:<use>')`), and from `planRegion` `planned`, `driven0`/`driven1` (the circuit and the sprint drive the spine), `dir0`/`dir1` (1 entry to exit, -1 back) and `special`.

## The mandate

`weekendPreference(ctx, base, closed)` merges into both finders' `RoutePreference` in `world.ts planRoads` (generalizing the old Underground preference): a route that drives the spine end to end is rated above any other (the flavour's own wish stays as the lesser rating), the finders start at the entry and the exit (`anchors`, `via`), the spine's pieces weigh `spineBias` (0.3) of their length and the ring's `ringBias` (2.5) times theirs, so a route turns in along the approach street instead of running round. `spineDirection(g, nodes, closed, region)` is the test: the spine's nodes in order (either way), the approach street's node before and the other's after, and on a sprint the spine off the start and `sprintTailClear` (262 m) plus the finish's node clearance from the finish, so the grid and the finish line stand outside. `planRoads`' `pick` asks every alternative for both a shortcut and the spine; when none has both, the spine outranks the shortcut and up to `routeTries` (24) more variants are drawn. `weekendKeepOut` holds the polygon against the route shortcuts and the car parks. `resolveEvent` promises the special only when `planned` and `special` are 1 (both routes drive it); a seed that carves no region promises nothing, and a region the routes miss is still built whole (never half a region), just not promised.

The waypoint race (`createRoute`) builds two in three of its attempts out from the entry and the exit as consecutive waypoints and likes a course that drives the spine. Drag and drift are exempt.

Measured over the 30 seeds of each kind from 2026-10-31 (`npx tsx scripts/weekendRate.ts 30 --waypoint`): regions carved, planned, circuit and sprint through the spine and specials promised 30/30 for every kind; waypoint courses through it 30/30 (Scrapyard), 29/30 (Rail Yard), 30/30 (Underground).

## Progress and lost

`CORRIDOR_AREAS` holds the three kinds: inside the region's polygon `updateLost` (sim/race.ts) counts a car as on the corridor, so it is never reset however far from the spine it runs. Progress: the plain projection onto the route is not enough (a car 100 m to the side on the far line of a three-cell region projects onto whatever route segment is nearest, the approach street behind it included), so on weekend seeds `regionProgress` (weekendRegion.ts, the car park's pattern) places a car anywhere in the polygon at the nearest point of the spine in driving order, mapped linearly onto the route stretch from the entry to the exit: progress runs on monotonically on every line, the wrong-way check reads the spine's direction, and outside the polygon the plain projection takes over where it ends. `tests/weekend.test.ts` drives a car along a line more than 90 m off the spine on the first Scrapyard day (a third of it beyond `LOST.lateral` of the route) with no reset, no wrong way and progress that never goes back. Weekday ticks do no extra work (`world.weekend` is null).

## The lines contract

A weekend module implements `DistrictModule.lines(area): FeatureLine[]` (types.ts), a pure function of the area like `passes`, two to six lines, the spine first:

```ts
interface FeatureLine {
  id: string;                    // stable within the area: 'spine', 'dock', 'canyon'
  name: string;                  // 'The trench floor'
  kind: 'spine' | 'alt';         // exactly one spine
  path: Vec2[];                  // entry junction to exit junction (path[0] = region.path[0], last = the exit), every inner point inside the polygon
  width: number;                 // clear width (m)
  level: 'ground' | 'deck' | 'below';
  surface: Surface;              // what most of it drives on
  difficulty: 1 | 2 | 3;         // safe, quick with a catch, risky
}
```

`spineLine(region, name, surface, level, difficulty)` makes the spine's. The renderer may light them, the tests check them (2 to 6, one spine, ends on the junctions, inside the region), and the rivals will pick from them. **Rivals today** drive the spine (the route's line). The hook for a rival picking a line by personality is `sim/ai/driverLine.ts driverDetours`: it already lays per-driver detours down `route.shortcuts` and `route.quayCuts` (a `Detour` from one sample of the driver's line to another along a path). A region's alt line is the same shape: from the route position of the entry (`regionProgress`'s `sIn`) to the exit's, along `path`; the driver's aggression picks the difficulty (from `rollDrivers`, `RaceConfig.aiSeed`). It needs lines whose paths are drivable centre lines clear of every solid, which the minimal layouts' are not yet, so it is left for the AI pass.

## Surfaces

Firm ground is the rule inside a region: `regionGround(b, region, surface)` lays the module's base surface over the whole polygon at priority 4 (`quay` for all three today), with buildings excluded and the frontage opened. `offroad` is the exception. **`slick`** (types.ts `Surface` and `SurfacePhysics`, `drivingParams.json` `surface.slickGrip` 0.45 and `slickDrag` 0, car.ts) is the unpredictable patch: oil, mud, wet steel plates, a road's firmness and drag with the grip gone. Paint patches of it at priority 6 and up on the firm floor, never across the whole of a line, and give the renderer a hint to draw each (an invisible slick patch is unfair). It is a sim change only where a patch is painted.

## The fence and the entry

`regionFence(region, piece)` is the ring in pieces, open where the flares cross it (the approach street's half width plus `mouthClear`); `regionFenceWalls(b, region, look, thick)` stands a thin solid of the module's look on each piece (solid both sides, so no car is shoved through). The renderer draws the same pieces (the Scrapyard's from `scrapLayout(area).fences` and `posts`, the Rail Yard's as yardFence solids, the Underground's in `render/districts/underground.ts regionHoarding`). A module opens more of it where its ring frontage should face the region (filter the pieces before standing walls on them, and draw what you keep). No signs, no arrows: the approach street's width and asphalt carry on into the region through the gap in the fence.

## What an area module owns

- `carve(ctx)`: `if (!C.enabled || ctx.weekend?.kind !== '<kind>') return; carveRegion(ctx, { kind, shapes, spineWidth, straight?, lines? })`. Extra feature edges (a branch, a loop) go in the spec's `lines(g, spine, polygon)` callback: `addNode` / `addEdge(g, a, b, 'feature', { width, lanes: 2, sidewalk: 0 })` between spine nodes or new nodes, every edge inside the polygon (the framework throws otherwise), never joining a street outside. They are never closed off, no rail stands at their nodes, and the routes' preference ignores them.
- `plan(ctx)`: `const r = planRegion(ctx, '<kind>')`, then the layout from `r` (the spine's `path`, `polygon`, widths) into the area's params. Set `params.planned = 0` and `params.special = 0` if the layout cannot fit (nothing built, nothing promised). Draw from `deriveSeed(params.seed, ...)`, not `ctx.rng` (each lifecycle step gets a fresh copy of the module's stream).
- `build(ctx, area)`: `regionGround`, `regionFenceWalls` (or the module's own boundary), then walls with drawn looks, surfaces, height mods with exact gradients that fade to 0 at their boxes (the spine's elevation is the module's height mods: the asphalt drapes on the terrain), destructibles, hints.
- `lines(area)`: two to six `FeatureLine`s, the spine first.
- Its renderer (`render/districts/<kind>.ts`): every look, the region fence, the slick patches it paints, the flare's ground.
- `CITY.districts.<kind>` sizes, `tests/<kind>.test.ts`.
- Not theirs: `weekendRegion.ts`, `weekend.ts`, the shared files (`types.ts`, `index.ts`, `world.ts`, `route.ts`, `routeSearch.ts`, the render registry), the menu.
- Must not: delete or move streets outside `carve`, put a wall on a road (the spine is a road), stand a one-sided wall with drivable ground less than 4 m behind it, block the spine (it is the race's road and the rivals' line), leave reachable ground that is neither part of a line nor fenced off, or paint `offroad` over most of the region.

## The rubric every area meets

- 6 to 12 blocks (the region), 2 to 6 lines from the entry to the exit, the spine one of them.
- Mandatory for the circuit, the sprint and (where the course allows) the waypoint race; drag and drift exempt.
- Wide, straight entries: the approach street carries on into the region at its width, never a 90 degree mouth.
- Ramps and elevation changes, and paths above, below or beside each other with different obstacles.
- Firm ground with slick patches; dirt the exception.
- A jump, a banked curve, a destructible field on the risky line, a drift zone on the open ground (`sim/driftZones.ts`).
- Lines are read by light and surface, never by signs or floor arrows (no signposting shortcuts).
- Nothing a car can reach is barren or walled off by accident.

Any change to what a weekend seed plans changes that seed's replays and bests; weekday seeds are unaffected.

## The Underground (`underground.ts`, `undergroundShape.ts`, `render/districts/underground.ts`)

A half-built cut-and-cover expressway down the region's straight spine, at night: the spine is the trench floor five metres under grade, a third of it roofed and the roof a works plaza at grade, service roads either side, two Bailey bridges over the open trench, a utility gallery under the plaza, and three kickers that throw a car from the floor up onto the levels above. Tests: `tests/underground.test.ts`; sizes: `CITY.districts.underground` (every number with its unit, the kickers' measured speeds included).

**Where and the frame.** A region two cells across and four to six along (`UNDERGROUND_SHAPES`, 8 to 12 blocks, about 280 m by 540 to 840 m) on a straight spine `floor` (13 m) wide. `plan` keeps the spine's chord (`cx, cz, ux, uz`), `half`, the ramp tops `top` (`half - flare - endClear`: the ramps begin right after the flares, so the approach street drops into the trench straight ahead and the service roads peel off left and right at grade), the roof's half length `roof` (a third of the trench) and `dir` (`raceDirection`). `undergroundShape.ts undergroundSite(area)` lays everything else out in the **travel frame** (s along the race's direction, w to its right; a half turn of the frame when the race runs exit to entry, never a mirror), so the kickers, the windows and the gallery come in the same order and on the same side either way. A spine too short for a 60 m open floor either side of the roof (`minOpen`) is left unplanned and unpromised (no shape this module declares is).

**Across** (from the axis): the floor to |w| = 6.5 (the spine's asphalt under a concrete floor), the plinth to 8.1 (a solid: its floor face is the retaining wall, a kerb 0.7 m over grade; the terrain climbs from the floor to grade under its first 0.96 m, `climb`, and stays level under its back so every deck edge meets level ground), the service road (7 m of asphalt), the gravel verge (3 m, where the plant parks), and the hoarding that closes the works yards off. Under the roof on the right the wall is 3 m thick (to 9.5) and the gallery lies beyond it to 15.5, its outer wall to 16.5.

**Along** (mirrored about the middle): a 66 m ramp (eased, 10.1% at the steepest) down to the floor; the open floor (bridge A, the right kicker, the burst main before the portal); the roof over |s| <= R (the plaza kicker under the kicker's opening, the drop opening after it, the gallery under the right side); the open floor (bridge B, the left kicker, the bus); the ramp up. The demolished windows are on the right, on the lower half of each ramp.

**Profile** (`profileAt`, exact gradient): min(trench, gallery, passages) + kickers + bridge humps. The trench and the gallery never overlap; the passages meet both at their floors' level, so the min is continuous and its kinks lie under the walls. The gallery's floor ramps 1.5 m below the trench floor over 15 m after the first passage and back before the second. Every term fades to 0 inside `undergroundBox`.

**The six lines** (`undergroundLines`, entry to exit, each clear of every solid and prop by half its width at its own level, which the build enforces as it places the clutter and the test checks):

The line weaves off each kicker and the bus. |
| `left` The left service road | ground | 1 | At grade the whole way: trucks, excavators and light towers on the verge, sandbags and cones, the spoil heap with its `slick` mud across the road, the left kicker's landing, under the roof the plaza's edge. |

Beside the deck the plaza runs to the ring both sides: light masts in two rows, portakabins, a tower crane, material stacks and crane mats (all solids), pallets, drums and spools round them. |

**Decks.** The roof is `upper` DeckSpans (rectangles round the openings, `site.decks`) at grade over the trench and the gallery; each bridge is one at grade + 0.35; each plinth piece in the open carries one at the kerb's height, so a car flying over the retaining wall reads a flat kerb under it instead of the steep climb beneath the solid (without it the body's lean to the ground threw cars tens of metres up). The decks' heights are the terrain less the site's own profile (`gradeAt`), read once settleUnderground hands the terrain over. The terrain is the lower level everywhere, so the spine's asphalt drapes on the floor, the route, the AI and every reset drive it, and a body nearer a deck than the floor drives the deck. Surfaces: the deck is `road`; under it the floor is `concrete`, the gallery's wet patch `slick`.

**Walls** (all solids, look `trenchWall`, drawn by the renderer from the same site and hints; heights from `settleUnderground`): the plinth pieces (top: the kerb over grade in the open, a metre under grade beneath the roof and the bridges), the noses at the ramp tops, the kickers' steel flanks over their bevels (three pieces a side, each the heap's crest plus 0.4 m), the portals' headwalls and the openings' hoardings (`bottom` 2.5 m under the deck so a car in the tunnel passes beneath, `top` over it so a thrown car clears them; an opening's open end is only the slab's edge, its top just under the deck), the bridges' parapets (the same, from the bridge's deck), the gallery's outer and end walls, pipes and trays (a metre under grade), the bus, the plant, the plaza's pieces, the footbridges' abutments, and the hoardings: along the verges of the open sections and the ramps, diagonally to the ring at each end, and across to the ring at each end of the plaza (`yardHoardings`), plus the region's ring fence (`regionFenceWalls`).

**The kickers** (steel plates on a spoil heap: a 2.2 m lip at 28%, 3.6 m wide). Measured with the sim (a car held at speed to the lip, then coasting; `tests/underground.test.ts`):
- The plaza kicker, on the floor's left half under the kicker's opening, throws straight along the floor up through it: from 27 m/s it clears the opening's far slab edge and lands on the deck (30 m/s about 39 m past the lip, 40 m/s about 86 m, 45 m/s about 114 m, still on the roof); at 40 m/s it rises 6.4 m over the lip (8.6 m over the floor). Below 27 m/s the throw meets the slab's far edge and drops back into the tunnel.
- The side kickers (right in the first open section, left in the second) are turned about 5.7 degrees toward their side so the throw at 40 m/s lands mid service road: from 36 m/s the car clears the kerb and lands on the road (36: on its inner half, 40: mid road, 45: at its outer edge); at 30 to 33 m/s it meets the kerb or the plinth and falls back.
- The landing strips from the least speed to past the aim are kept clear of plant and clutter; the racing line keeps off every kicker and the bus (`undergroundClosures`, read by `sim/ai/racingLine.ts` beside the roadworks' closures), so the rivals run the free side of the floor.

**Moves between the levels** (measured): off a window at 12 to 28 m/s the car lands on the floor and drives on; into the drop opening at 12 to 30 m/s it falls to the floor and drives on; over a bridge at 15 to 30 m/s it hops (at most about 3 m/s up) and lands on the far road; a car can drop off the plaza's left edge only through the openings (the left plinth's top is the deck's edge, flush with the road).

**Rivals** drive the spine (the route's line); `undergroundLines` hands the five alternatives to the AI pass (`sim/ai/driverLine.ts driverDetours`), every path a drivable centre line clear of every solid and prop.

**Render** (one baked mesh for the concrete, one wash, one sign mesh; everything else in the kit's merged builders): measured with `npm run perf:scene -- --seed 20261107 --tier medium --camera route`, the renderer on against off: 1,230,976 against 1,198,623 triangles and 187 against 184 draw calls at the route camera (+32.4k, +3), and the scene's upper bound 17,971,394 against 17,909,210 (+62.2k, the whole region in view). Low drops the washes and thins the lamps and the yards' dressing.

**Shared files touched:** `config.ts` (the `underground` block), `sim/ai/racingLine.ts` (one line: the trench's closures beside the roadworks'), `render/camera/ceilings.ts` (the roof and the bridges as overheads, the roof a bore for the exposure).

**Not here.** A bank and a drift zone (the rubric's, for a later pass), rivals on the alternative lines.

## The Scrapyard

`scrapyard.ts` (build), `scrapyardPlan.ts` (frame and fit), `scrapyardLayout.ts` (the pure layout every reader shares: lines, stacks, solids, surfaces, clutter), `scrapyardShapes.ts` (the sheared frame and the height mods), `scrapyardFill.ts` (the scrap mountains), `scrapyardGates.ts` (the press), `banking.ts` (the bowls' dish and banks), `render/districts/scrapyard.ts` + `scrapyardParts.ts` + `scrapyardTextures.ts`. Sizes, and the measured jump speeds, are in `CITY.districts.scrapyard`. Tests: `tests/scrapyard.test.ts`.

**Site.** A region of 3 x 3, 4 x 3 or 3 x 4 cells (`SCRAPYARD_SHAPES`, about 400 to 630 m along the spine and 380 to 630 m across) on a sweeping spine `spineWidth` (16 m) wide, the haul road. The yard is laid out in a frame from the race's entry junction (`raceDirection`: the circuit's way along the spine, so both kickers face the way the race comes; the sprint may run it the other way): `u` along the spine's chord, `d` off the spine itself (a shear: P(u, d) = o + u U + (d + spineV(u)) N, so a line at constant `d` follows the spine's sweep, at most a dozen metres off its chord). `d > 0` is the shallower side of the spine at its middle (107 to 296 m deep over the first 20 Scrapyard days), where the Canyon, the Bale Deck and the Perimeter run; the Eight is on the deep side. ## The Rail Yard

**The rule every line keeps.** Everything a car can stop against keeps off every line's path by half the line's width plus `lineMargin` (`Clearance` in railYardPaths.ts): the spine and the lines are corridors in the book before anything is placed, and the layout places in that order: the shapes and the bridge's ramps (solid bodies), the drop's landing, the buildings, the crane, the containers, the tower, the signals, the floods, the piers, the clutter fields, the tracks and their freight (each car only where its box is clear, so the lines carve their own gaps through the consists), the clutter beside every line. `tests/railYard.test.ts` samples every line every 2 m against `World.collideCircle` at the line's own level (the deck's on the bridge) and every prop, on five seeds.

**The overbridge's two levels** (`railYardBridge.ts`, the highway's `upper` DeckSpan form): each chunk of the centre line is a span whose `upper` writes the roadway's absolute height and exact gradient; the ramps' spans are `solid` (an embankment between retaining walls), the deck's are not, so a car nearer the ground than the deck drives the yard under it. Heights come from the finished terrain (`settleRailYard`): a ground profile along the centre line (the highest ground across the roadway and 2 m either side every 8 m, smoothed over 40 m, never under that highest ground) plus 6 m times the ramps' eased share (9.5% peak climb over the ground; the city's own roll adds to the absolute grade on a hilly seed). Ramp parapets: Wall.top 1.1 m over the roadway; deck parapets: Wall.bottom 2.6 m under it; piers and the abutments at the ramps' tops: Wall.top 1.2 m under it. Every yard solid (and every railcar, each its own solid) has Wall.top at its own ground plus its height, so the deck passes over the freight beneath it and a car flying higher passes over too.

**The jumps** (measured with the sim, `tests/railYard.test.ts`, `C.hump.measured`, `C.kicker.measured`):
- The hump: speed at the crest 18 m/s just clears the fall (lands 18.5 m on), 30 m/s lands 37 m on, 42 m/s 61 m on; the landing is clear to 72 m.
- The kicker: a ballast heap under steel plates (1.9 m lip at 28%) in the hump band beside ramp P, aimed 15 degrees in toward it and placed so a 30 m/s throw comes down on the ramp's centre line a little over its surface. 24 to 34 m/s land on the climbing ramp and stay on it (36 and up clear its far parapet); slower falls short against its retaining wall. It throws a car off the hump lead up onto the bridge. It is beside the climbing ramp rather than the far (descending) one on purpose: a throw beside a ramp that falls away ahead crosses its edge uphill of where it lands, so it would have to clear a parapet higher than its own apex.
