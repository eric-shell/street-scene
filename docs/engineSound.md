# Engine sound

How the engine voices are made, what each of the first four engines is modelled on, and the plan for the rest. Code: `src/audio/engineSynth.ts` (the pure synth and the program built from a car's `EngineSpec`), `src/audio/engineWorklet.ts` (runs it on the audio thread), `src/audio/engineAudio.ts` (the voice: silencer convolution, intake, turbo, levels), `src/audio/engineAccessories.ts` (the turbo's boost model and sequential handover, surge flutter, wastegate chatter, exhaust flaps, fan and gear whine as pure code the voice and the tests share). Tests: `tests/engineVoice.test.ts`, `e2e/engine.spec.ts`.

## The model

The structure is Ange Yaghi's **Engine Simulator** (github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim, MIT; `src/synthesizer.cpp`, `src/piston_engine_simulator.cpp::writeToSynthesizer`) without its gas simulation. What that program showed is that an engine's character is mostly *when* and *how strongly* each cylinder's exhaust pulse reaches the tailpipe, not the waveform of a single pulse. So:

1. **Firing schedule.** Every layout has its real firing order and bank assignment (`SCHEDULE` in `engineSynth.ts`): a cylinder fires at `k / n` of the combustion cycle (720° for a four stroke, 360° per rotor).
2. **Exhaust paths.** Each cylinder's pulse travels its header primary to the collector; the arrival delay is the extra length over the shortest primary at 343 m/s, and the amplitude falls as 1 / length² (engine-sim's rule, against a 1 m reference). Header style is per car: tubular headers are nearly equal, a turbo's log manifold is short but position dependent, a boxer's "unequal" headers put the left bank half a metre further away.
3. **Pulse.** A blowdown rise (raised cosine, ~0.012 of the cycle), an exponential exhaust stroke decay and a scavenging dip, as a wavetable over cycle phase, so pulses shorten in time as revs rise. A turbine downstream widens the rise and eats the dip (`smear`). Each firing gets a shot to shot amplitude variance, largest at idle (the lope); the limiter drops firings at random.
4. **Per pipe post chain** (engine-sim's `renderAudio`): band limit at 1.5 to 2.6 kHz (their 1.9 kHz input filter), a wandering sample delay (their `JitterFilter`), a 10 Hz DC block, then `edge × derivative + pressure × (air × lowpassed noise + (1 − air))`. The derivative is what a pipe end radiates and carries the tone; the noise term is the turbulence that makes it breathe. Engine-sim runs almost pure derivative (`dF_F_mix = 0.01` of a `/dt` derivative is a ×440 weight), we keep `edge` per layout.
5. **Leveler and loudness.** A power follower sets a slow gain so the raw voice sits at the same RMS at idle and at the redline, whatever the train's crest factor; the voice applies load, rpm and distance on top. Equal RMS is not equal loudness (a bright six with throttle bodies reads louder than a smeared turbo at the same RMS), so `buildProgram` measures each program through a model of the main thread chain (`estimateLoudness`: the spectra of 0.12 s of both tips at 3500 rpm full throttle times the silencer response, the presence peak and the top roll-off, plus the intake and body through their bands, K-weighted as LUFS does since A-weighting discounts the bass an engine lives in; about ten milliseconds, once per voice) and sets `trim` so every program lands on `LOUDNESS_REF`. Nothing is hand levelled: a new layout, silencer or intake is matched the moment it exists, and `tests/engineVoice.test.ts` runs the full chain with the real convolution to check the cars stay within 2 dB and none is mono. The chain's numbers (`CHAIN`) live in `engineSynth.ts` so the model and the nodes cannot drift apart.
6. **Tailpipes.** Every voice renders two tips, left and right. A twin pipe car (`pipes: 2`) puts a bank on each, with the second pipe 5 cm further: summed in mono the two banks' trains would otherwise cancel exactly (the chain is linear), which real cars avoid because you hear two tailpipes and they are never equal. A single exhaust sends the one train through two tips with their own turbulence and jitter and a 0.3 ms path difference, so it is as wide as a twin pipe car instead of a mono point. Heard from one pipe a cross-plane V8 bank measures +2.5 dB uneven, a flat-plane bank −14 dB, an inline six's three −21 dB.
7. **Silencer.** The sum is convolved (`ConvolverNode`) with an impulse response we render ourselves (`renderImpulse`): a unit direct path, the pipe column's half wave modes for the system length, the silencer chamber and a tailpipe ring for sporty ones as resonances given by Q and their boost over the direct path (a decaying sine of amplitude A and time constant τ has gain Aτ/2 at its frequency, so amplitudes are tiny and a +6 dB mode is a +6 dB mode), and a diffuse noise tail sized by its energy against the direct path. The first version sized the sines like impulses, which made every mode a +40 dB peak that sub-harmonic content lit up at random; levels only became predictable once the modes were physical. Engine-sim ships recorded responses; ours are synthesized so everything stays runtime generated and nothing of theirs is redistributed.
8. **Intake and body.** The worklet also renders the intake (a softer pulse half a cycle from the exhaust one, scaled by throttle^1.5, through a trumpet band pass on the main thread: individual throttle bodies honk, a plenum murmurs) and the valvetrain tick (a 1.5 ms noise burst at each firing, loudest with the throttle closed).
9. **Events.** The sim's gear change cut (`shiftTimer`) zeroes the load; rpm pinned at the redline with the pedal down turns the limiter on; overrun pops come from the model below.

### Overrun pops and the exhaust flash

Real pops are unburnt fuel lighting in a hot exhaust, so the voice keeps two states (`OVERRUN` in `engineAudio.ts`) and the sim knows nothing about them:

- **Heat** rises under load at revs with a 4 s time constant and cools over 20 s. A cold engine barely pops on the overrun; one that has been driven hard does.
- **Fuel** is dumped into the pipe on a lift from above 45 % of the redline, the amount rising steeply with how high the lift was (and ×1.3 for a turbo running rich under boost); it burns off over 1.2 s, and the pedal takes it back in 0.15 s.
- **Three ways in.** On the limiter (rpm within 150 of the redline with the pedal down, which the sim holds in top gear and the garage bounces on) the cut dumps mixture every few firings: a stream of 3 to 9 events per second by silencer, mostly crackles. A high rev upshift's cut pops with chance `0.35 × freedom × heat`. On the overrun the chance is `pops × 0.45 × fuel × heat × dt` per second, where `pops` is the silencer's freedom (quiet 0.3, sport 1.2, straight 2.5, turbo ×0.6): a hot sport car pops on roughly a quarter of its lifts from the top, rarely from half revs, a quiet turbo car one in twenty. 45 % of overrun events are a single bang (a 40 ms crack with a 90 Hz thump), the rest a burst of two to four crackles 30 to 80 ms apart.
- Pops join the exhaust **after** the leveler at their own peak (about 0.12 a crackle, 0.27 a bang, against a levelled tip's 0.05 RMS; soft clipped), so the leveler cannot duck them and they read the same whatever the engine is doing; the silencer response still colours them (its pipe modes lift the bang's thump, which is why the thump is kept small). Measured at the bus a bang peaks about 6 dB over the engine's own peaks.
- The same event fires the sound in the worklet (`port.postMessage({ pop })`, `EngineSynth.pop`) and `EngineVoice.onPop`, which the race session and the garage route to `CarView.popFlash`: an orange additive cone at that tailpipe (the nitrous flame's position) that bursts out and dims over 70 ms for a crackle, 160 ms for a bang. No light is added (a new point light would recompile every lit material); the flash is emissive only.
- **Pops & bangs** (Tune tab, `visual.popsBangs`, 0 to 2, 1 stock): scales the chance of every overrun event above (`EngineVoice.popRate`) and the render-side shift flames and crackle in `exhaustFx.ts` (by a per-tick hash), so sight and sound agree. It is cosmetic: part of the visual build and share code (`pb`), never read by the sim. Past 1 it only raises chances; a silencer that does not pop (`pops` 0) stays quiet.

### Grain

Three things made the first version hiss above 4 kHz, and all were sources rather than something to filter: the valvetrain tick fired a highpassed noise burst at every firing (350 a second for a six at 7000 rpm, a continuous crackle), so it now fires at most every 25 ms and fades out by three quarters of the redline; the jitter noise was nearly white (engine-sim's 10 kHz cutoff), so the wander is now band limited to 600 Hz and reads as the pipe moving; and the turbulence noise was 2 kHz wide, now 1.2 kHz. The pulse wavetable went from 2048 to 8192 entries so the derivative of its interpolation no longer steps at idle. On top of that the voice rolls off above 6.5 kHz (`VOICE.top`), since nothing but grain lives there. Decel is only 3 dB under full throttle now (`0.7 + 0.3 × load`) and the leveler recovers in 0.15 s after a lift, so the overrun, where the pops and the silencer are heard, no longer drops away.

Everything is deterministic from a seed (xorshift); each voice gets its own seed so rivals never phase lock with the player. Rivals run a `lite` program: half the silencer response, no intake or valvetrain.

Cost, measured headless at 48 kHz in Node: 5 to 13 ms of CPU per second of audio per voice (about 1 %), on the audio thread, not the frame. The valvetrain and firing options each add under 0.5 ms per second of audio when a car uses them, and nothing when it does not: the cam changeover blends a second wavetable (one more interpolated lookup per cylinder per sample, only while the cam is in: 6.2 to 6.7 ms on a four at 6,500 rpm, 6.5 below the switch), the odd-fire V10 and the VR6 table only change the program's numbers (9.1 ms, 7.3 ms), the rotor's lope and rasp are per block and per firing (5.6 to 5.8 ms at idle). The Kombi, measured with them: 6.7 ms. The turbo and accessory behaviours add nothing to the worklet (see their section for what each costs on the main thread). The convolution is native. Four voices in a race are well inside budget on a laptop; phones have not been measured yet.

## Data

The manifest's `engine` block gained an optional `voice` (vocabulary in `src/shared/carSpec.ts`, validated by `npm run validate` and the tests):

| Field | Values | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| `headers` | `equal`, `unequal` | Unequal: the second bank's primaries run `headerSpread` further, so its pulses arrive late and weaker (the boxer rumble, the VR6 growl). |
| `headerSpread` | 0 to 1 (m) | How much further, with unequal headers. Defaults: 0.5 (a boxer's crossover under the engine), 0.3 for a VR6. |
| `pipes` | `1`, `2` | Two keeps each bank, or each half of an inline six, on its own pipe, so uneven per bank patterns reach the tailpipe. |
| `crossover` | `none`, `h`, `x` | Between twin pipes: an H-pipe hands 20 % of each bank's pulse to the other pipe, an X-pipe 45 %, evening out the per pipe pattern (smoother, raspier); nothing with one pipe. |
| `muffler` | `quiet`, `sport`, `straight` | System length, band limit, pops per lift, and the impulse response (long low modes vs short bright ring). |
| `intake` | `plenum`, `itb` | Throttle bodies: a loud resonant honk that follows the pedal. |
| `crank` | `cross`, `flat`, `oddfire` | V8: cross-plane banks fire 180-270-180-90 (burble), flat-plane 180-180-180-180 (scream). V10 only, `oddfire`: the Viper's 90° V on a 72° crank, firing 1-10-9-4-3-6-5-8-7-2 at 90° and 54° alternating (see below); the plain V10 stays even fire at 72°. Validation rejects `oddfire` on other layouts. |
| `blowerLobes` | `3`, `4`, `5`, `6`, `8` | Supercharged only: lobe passes per rotor turn, the whine's order. A twin screw's male rotor has 3 to 5 lobes; a Roots rotor counts twice, so 2, 3 and 4 lobe rotors give 4, 6 and 8 (the 2013 GT500's Eaton TVS 2300 is 4 lobe). 7 is no real blower, so it is out. At 8 the whine is rpm / 60 × ratio × 8, about 2.1 kHz at 6,500 rpm with the 2.4 ratio: still inside the band the voice rolls off at (6.5 kHz), the fourth partial at 8.3 kHz is just trimmed. |
| `camRpm`, `camStep` | 4,000 to 9,000 rpm; 0 to 1 | Cam changeover (VTEC and kin). Above `camRpm` on the pedal (at least 0.4; it lets go below 0.1) a second, high-lift cam engages, and lets go again 150 rpm below the switch, so a rev hovering at the boundary does not chatter. In sound: the pulse gets a sharper blowdown rise (×0.5) and a shorter exhaust stroke (×0.8) through a second wavetable the first blends into, the band limit opens by 60 %, the voice is 30 % louder after the leveler (which would otherwise iron the step away) and the intake honk swells by 150 %, all scaled by `camStep` (default 0.7 when `camRpm` is set; 1 is a Honda VTEC) and followed with a 12 ms lag. Absent means nothing: below the switch the output is sample for sample the plain voice. See the cam section below. |
| `idleLope`, `apexRasp` | 0 to 1; 0 to 2 | Rotary only. `idleLope`: the port overlap lope at idle (depth, 1 is the 13B-REW). `apexRasp`: the apex seal rasp, a noise burst at each rotor face's firing (1 is the 13B-REW). Both absent means none. See the rotary section below. |
| `diverter` | `vent`, `recirculating`, `flutter` | Turbo only, the sound of a lift at boost. `vent` (default): an atmospheric blow-off valve, a sharp highpassed chuff that kills the spool. `recirculating`: a stock diverter or bypass valve, a softer band passed "pssh" (6 dB lighter, 20 ms attack, the band sweeping 4.2 to 1.8 kHz as the charge air bleeds back) with the turbine keeping most of its speed, so the whistle falls away instead of dropping. `flutter`: no valve at all, the lift is only the compressor's surge flutter (below). |
| `flutter` | 0 to 1 | Turbo only. Surge flutter ("stu-tu-tu") added to a valve's release, at a hard lift only (pedal over 0.85 at boost over 0.6), as a share of the full flutter. 0, the default, is none; `diverter: 'flutter'` is always 1 and flutters on every lift the valve would have blown off for. |
| `wastegate` | 0 to 2 | Turbo only. Wastegate chatter: a run of 6 to 11 metallic ticks, 16 to 34 ms apart and fading, as the gate opens on a lift from boost over 0.5, as a multiple of the reference level. 0, the default, is none. Not on rivals' `lite` voices. |
| `handoverRpm`, `spoolRpm`, `preboostRpm`, `handoverDip` | 2,500 to 6,500; 1,000 to 4,000; 2,000 to 6,000; 0 to 0.5 | Turbo only. A sequential twin turbo: the primary reaches full boost at `spoolRpm` (1,800 when missing) and holds it, the second turbine pre-spools from `preboostRpm` (500 under the handover when missing) and its output joins at `handoverRpm`, where the boost dips by `handoverDip` (a fraction: 0.2 is 10 psi down to 8) and then surges. `handoverRpm` switches it on; the others need it and must satisfy spool < preboost <= handover. Without `handoverRpm` the boost model is the plain follower, bit for bit. Sound only: the sim's torque is untouched. See the sequential twin turbo section. |
| `flaps` | 0 to 1 | Active exhaust flaps: how much quieter and duller the silencer is with the flaps shut (1 is 9 dB down and rolled off at 1.4 kHz), open on load or revs. 0, the default, is none. No shipped car has it. See the flaps section. |
| `fanRatio` | 0.5 to 3 | Air-cooled engines: the cooling fan's turns per crank turn. Adds the fan's blade pass whine, locked to rpm. Absent means liquid cooled. No shipped car has it. |
| `gearWhine` | 0 to 2 | Gearbox whine, as a multiple of the reference (a straight cut dog box at full load). Needs the gearbox output shaft speed (`EngineInput.driveRpm`); 0, the default, is none. No shipped car has it. |
| `manifold` | `split3` | Inline six only. A cast exhaust manifold's runner table instead of the layout's ramp: `split3` is a twin turbo manifold split front three / rear three, each trio converging on its own turbine, the middle runner short and the outer pair 0.05 m longer (`MANIFOLD` in `engineSynth.ts`). Absent keeps the ramp (0 / 0.05 / 0.10 m down each bank, the Zenith's). |
| `howl` | 0 to 1 | The high-rpm rasp (`HOWL`): above 0.55 of the redline, with the pedal down (30 % left with it up), the pulses sharpen (a second wavetable with a 0.55 times shorter blowdown rise and 0.8 times shorter exhaust stroke, blended in), the band limit opens by 50 % and the derivative share rises by 50 %, the intake honk by 50 %, all scaled by the field (smoothstep over rpm, an 80 ms follower). The leveler holds the RMS, so what changes is where the energy sits: toward 1 to 3 kHz. 0, the default, is none; below the onset the output is sample for sample the plain voice. See the Hinode Horizon section. |
| `twinWhistle` | 0 to 0.1 | Turbo only, not with `handoverRpm`: a parallel twin turbo's whistle as two sines this far apart as a fraction of pitch (0.015 is 1.5 %), centred on the single turbo's pitch, each at 0.72 of its level (`TWIN_WHISTLE`; the power stays the same). Absent is one tone. |
| `whoosh` | 0 to 2 | Turbo only, the induction whoosh: noise through a band that climbs from 0.7 to 3.0 kHz with boost, growing as boost^1.5, at 0.12 of the reference level times this (0, the default, is none). 1 is the K24 on a stock Audi S2. Not on rivals' `lite` voices. |

Defaults by layout are `LAYOUT_VOICE`; turbo engines default to the quiet silencer, the rest to sport. A layout can also carry a runner table (`runners`: extra primary length per cylinder position along the head, `levels`: a pulse level per position), for a manifold whose runners the ramp and `stagger` cannot express; the inline five (`FIVE_LOG`) and the VR6 (`VR6_HEAD`) have one, and `stagger` is now unused by any layout. A car can also pick a named table with `voice.manifold` (`MANIFOLD`: `split3`, the RB26DETT's front three / rear three), which replaces the layout's ramp for that car only; the generic six, and with it the Zenith, keeps its ramp. The turbo voice's numbers (whistle, whoosh, vent and recirculating valves, the spool constants) are the `TURBO` table in `engineSynth.ts`, shared by the nodes and the tests' offline renders. Everything added after it (`SEQUENTIAL`, `FLUTTER`, `CHATTER`, `FLAPS`, `FAN`, `GEAR_WHINE`, the `TurboModel` boost model and the pulse schedules) is in `engineAccessories.ts`, pure and shared by the nodes and the tests.

Per-car donor data (engine, intake, exhaust, drivetrain, sound character and the voice fields to use): [carEngines.md](carEngines.md).

## The garage engines

### Wendt Strom: VW VR6, naturally aspirated (Golf R32 Mk4)

- Reference: 3.2 l VR6 (BFH/BDB), 15° narrow V sharing one head, firing order 1-5-3-6-2-4, 84.0 x 95.9 mm, 24 valves with variable cam timing, 10.9 to 11.3:1, Bosch Motronic ME 7.1.1. One manifold per three cylinders but runners of two lengths, as the narrow V staggers them.
- The sound: even 120° firing, but odd cylinders sit on one side of the narrow V, so 1-5-3-6-2-4 fires three from one side and then three from the other. The far side's runners reach round the V and run longer, so its three pulses arrive late and weaker: a beat once a cycle under the six's even note, the hollow, gravelly growl the VR6 is known for (the "Chewbacca" sound). The runners also taper within a side (see the runner table below). No turbines, so the pulses stay sharp.
- Spec: `VR6`, `na`, `voice: { headers: 'unequal', headerSpread: 0.35, pipes: 1, muffler: 'sport' }` (the R32's twin centre tips come from one system). The far side's pulses arrive 1.0 ms late at about 0.7 of the gain.
- Runner table (`VR6_HEAD`, replacing the scalar `stagger` of 0.07 m on every other runner of a side, which was a guess): one extra primary length per cylinder 1 to 6 along the head, so the schedule's slot is now the cylinder's place on the head (odd cylinders the near row, even the far) and the table follows the zigzag. Values 0.07 / 0.035 / 0 / 0 / 0.035 / 0.07 m (outer runners longest, tapering to the collector at the middle), the far row's reach round the V on top as `headerSpread` 0.35. Sources are thin and the table is our estimate: Wikipedia's VR6 article (docs/carEngines.md) says only that port lengths differ between the banks and that cam overlap, lift and runner length compensate so both banks make similar power; no runner length is published; engine-sim ships no VR6 (its assets/engines listing is the Audi, BMW, Chevrolet, Kohler and Ange's two video sets), so there is no level table either, and `levels` is left unset, since that same article is the reason not to favour a row. Resulting pulses: near row 0 to 0.20 ms late at 0.92 to 1.0, far row 1.0 to 1.2 ms at 0.62 to 0.67.
- Measured (sub orders, cycle harmonics 1 to 5 over the 6th; unevenness as for the Kombi): before (alternating stagger) -12.1 / -8.4 / -0.2 dB at 900 / 3,000 / 6,000 rpm, now -13.3 / -8.8 / -2.3, against an inline six's -16.9 / -22.4 / -21.9. The totals barely moved (the old guess was in the right range), as for the Kombi: the near/far split is the beat once a cycle, and the table makes the runners real data per cylinder instead of one scalar. Loudness: the manifest cars still land within 2 dB; cost unchanged (the table only changes the program's numbers). `tests/engineVoice.test.ts` asserts the table's order and 10 dB more sub orders than the six at 3,000 and 6,000 rpm.
- Still to add: tune `headerSpread` and the taper against a recording of the R32, and the intake honk of its large plenum from 4000 up.

### Kazama Rotor: Mazda 13B-REW twin rotor, sequential twin turbo (RX-7 FD)

- Reference: 1308 cc two rotor Wankel, sequential twin turbos (the secondary joins near 4,500 rpm), 9.0:1, redline 8,000. Each rotor fires once per eccentric shaft turn, so two rotors fire as often as a four cylinder at the same rpm, but evenly and with no valvetrain.
- The sound: a smooth, high buzz from even firing and short exhaust ports, rounded by the turbines; the brap at idle comes from port overlap.
- Spec: `R2`, `turbo`, `voice: { pipes: 1, muffler: 'sport', idleLope: 1, apexRasp: 1 }`.
- Idle lope (`LOPE`): at idle the intake and exhaust ports overlap and burnt gas leaks back, so some strokes fire weak, in irregular groups (docs/carEngines.md: the 13B-REW's stock lope is "mild", a bridge port's is worse; character is not a sourced figure, so the numbers are tuned by ear and by measurement). Modelled as a chance that a firing is weak (0.3 of its level at depth 1) that two slow incommensurate waves (2.3 and 3.7 Hz) push between 0.15 and 0.7, times a window that is full under 1,200 rpm, gone by 2,500 and 85 % cut by a full pedal. Measured, the spread of the level from one firing to the next (std over mean of the RMS per firing interval): 0.22 at 850 rpm and 0.35 at 1,500 (0.09 and 0.10 without), the same as without at 2,500, 3,000 and 6,500; the 0.25 s window level at idle varies by more than 15 %, bursts a few times a second.
- Apex seal rasp (`RASP`): the three seals of each rotor sweep the housing and chatter, so every rotor face's firing gets a 1.2 ms noise burst, squared decay, at 0.35 × rpm^1.5 / redline^1.5 × (0.35 + 0.65 pedal), ±30 % per burst. It is rpm locked (its envelope has its line at the firing rate), goes out of the body output (the 2.2 kHz band the valvetrain tick uses, past the silencer: seal chatter is mechanical, not exhaust) and so is not heard on rivals' lite voices. Measured, body RMS 0.008 at 3,000 rpm and 0.039 at 6,500 against 0.002 and 0 without. The loudness model includes it at 3,500 rpm, so the trim moved 0.767 to 0.758 (-0.1 dB).
- Cost: the lope is a handful of operations per block and per firing, the rasp one noise draw per sample while a burst lasts: 5.56 to 5.76 ms of CPU per second of audio at idle, 5.72 to 5.73 at 6,500 rpm.
- Sequential twin turbo (`spoolRpm 1800`, `preboostRpm 4000`, `handoverRpm 4500`, `handoverDip 0.2`, sourced: docs/carEngines.md) with a recirculating valve and `flutter 0.3` (the section's "relief-valve flutter" gap): see the sequential twin turbo section.
- Still to add: the exhaust pops of the overrun on the tubular manifold.

### Brandt Kombi: Audi 2.2 20V turbo inline five (S2 Avant donor)

- Reference: the 2,226 cc 20 valve turbocharged inline five of the 1992-95 Audi S2 (engine codes 3B, then ABY from October 1992: 230 PS / 169 kW at 5,900 rpm, 350 N·m from 1,950 to 3,000 rpm), one KKK K24 turbo on a short log manifold, firing order 1-2-4-5-3, quattro with a Torsen centre differential and the 01E six speed. Sources: Wikipedia's Audi S2 (the 2,226 cc five, codes 3B and ABY, 230 PS and 350 N·m with 380 N·m on overboost, 246 km/h) and a catalogue listing of the ABY at 230 PS / 5,900 rpm and 350 N·m / 1,950 rpm; the 01E ratios are the RS2's (CRB) 3.500 / 1.889 / 1.320 / 1.034 / 0.857 / 0.711 on the 4.111 final drive, the S2's CGR box being listed with the same (forum sources disagree on its fifth and sixth).
- The sound: even 144° firing, so the base note is the 2.5 order (rpm / 60 × 2.5) and the warble is everything off it: the half orders (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0) and the sidebands at 3.5 and 4.0 that the cylinders' unequal paths to the turbine put around it. The headers stay `equal` (the log manifold is the runner table below). One turbine smears the pulses, and the stock S2 has one exhaust system ending in twin tips, so the voice is one pipe (`pipes: 1`, two tips rendered from the one train) behind the quiet (turbo default) silencer.
- Runner data (`FIVE_LOG` in `engineSynth.ts`, a table per cylinder 1 to 5, replacing the old alternating `stagger` of 0.03 m, which was a guess). Two sources, and what they do not say:
  - Layout: the 3B / AAN / ABY cast log manifold feeds the one turbo from the middle of the head; cylinders 2, 3 and 4 run straight into it while the runners of 1 and 5 "rise up to connect to the exhaust manifold just under the wastegate flange" (quattroworld.com forum, "OE 3B, AAN, ABY and ADU Exhaust Components: a consolidation of info", and the same thread on s2forum.com; the RS2 manifold drops those risers). No runner length is published, so the table is our estimate: extra primary over the middle runner of 0.18 m for 1 and 5, 0.045 m for 2 and 4, none for 3, on the 0.15 m turbo log base. That is 0.525 ms late at a pulse level of 0.75 for the outer pair, 0.131 ms at 0.74 for 2 and 4 (0.8 of engine-sim's level times the path loss), 1.0 for 3.
  - Levels: AngeTheGreat's engine-sim five (`assets/engines/audi/i5.mr`, the same engine in `atg-video-1/07_audi_i5.mr`; MIT, fetched Oct 2026) fires 1-2-4-5-3 at 144° with cylinders 1-3-5 on one exhaust system at audio volume 1.0 and 2-4 on a second at 0.8, every primary 10 in (0.254 m), one impulse response, and a per cylinder piston blowby of 0.2 / 0.6 / 0.6 / 0.4 / 0.4. It has no runner lengths, so it gives the levels only (1.0 / 0.8 / 1.0 / 0.8 / 1.0, multiplied with the path loss above, so cylinder 3 is the one strong pulse of a cycle). The blowby is combustion leakage, not exhaust path, and is not used.
- Measured (`unevenness()`: energy at the cycle harmonics off the firing grid over on it, dB; sub orders: harmonics 1 to 4 over the 2.5 order), headless at 48 kHz, full throttle except idle (900 rpm, 10 % throttle):

  | | idle | 3,000 rpm | 6,000 rpm |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Kombi before (stagger 0.03) | -10.4 | -7.6 | -2.1 |
  | Kombi now | -10.7 | -7.0 | -2.0 |
  | same engine as a turbo I6 on one pipe | -14.1 | -11.8 | -6.7 |
  | turbo I4 | -10.4 | -11.7 | -6.8 |
  | turbo boxer on unequal headers | -4.2 | +2.0 | +0.9 |
  | sub orders: Kombi before / now | -18.7 / -11.6 | -15.8 / -14.4 | -13.0 / -13.6 |
  | sub orders: turbo I6 | -18.8 | -18.6 | -15.6 |

  The total did not move (the old guess was in the right range); what changed is where the energy is and why. ## Valvetrain and firing options

### Cam changeover

Honda's VTEC swaps from a mild cam to a high-lift one at a set rpm, and what the ear gets is a step: a deep growl turns into a metallic wail. `voice.camRpm` (with `camStep`) models it as above (`CAM` in `engineSynth.ts`); it needs the pedal because the ECU wants load, and it has 150 rpm of hysteresis. Switch rpm are sourced in docs/carEngines.md: NSX C30A 5,800 (strong, `camStep` 1), S2000 F22C1 about 6,000 (strong), Civic Si B16A2 5,600 (strong). Those cars are only planned, so none of the built cars sets the field yet.

Measured on a four cylinder at 6,500 rpm with step 1 and a switch at 5,800: the 3 to 6 kHz share of the exhaust +8 dB (4 to 6 kHz +8, 3 to 4 kHz +6.6), RMS +1.5 dB, intake 2.5 times; at 5,000 rpm sample for sample identical to a car without the field. The switch itself takes a time constant of 12 ms, per 128 sample block.

Deliberately not modelled: continuous phasers (BMW VANOS, Toyota VVT-i, Mitsubishi MIVEC, Porsche Varioram's manifold switch). They move torque around smoothly and are not audible as an event, so a step would be wrong. The intake manifold switches (Varioram, the Golf's two stage change near 4,000 to 4,500, the NSX's shutter at 4,800) would be a second, milder use of the same machinery if ever wanted.

### Odd-fire V10

The Viper's V10 (docs/carEngines.md; firing order 1-10-9-4-3-6-5-8-7-2 from one site, the intervals unsourced there) is `crank: 'oddfire'`. The intervals are derived from the crank geometry and then checked against the firing order: five crank pins 72° apart, each carrying a rod per bank, with the banks 90° apart, so the two cylinders of a pin fire 90° apart and the five pins' pairs 720° / 5 = 144° apart. That makes 90° and 54° alternating (90 + 54 = 144). The firing order's pairs 1-10, 9-4, 3-6, 5-8, 7-2 are each an odd and an even cylinder, which is what a pin shared by the two banks needs (odd cylinders on one bank). It confirms the doc's alternation; the doc's own 72° / 108° guess was wrong. Each bank alone (one pipe each) fires evenly every 144°, so the lump only exists where the pipes join: with `pipes: 2` and no crossover each tailpipe is a smooth five and the unevenness is in the sum, which the ear gets from behind the car. Schedule times are explicit crank fractions (`V10_ODD`, a third value in the schedule entry).

Measured, summed to one ear (unevenness: energy at the cycle harmonics off the firing grid over on it; sub orders: cycle harmonics 1 to 9 over the 10th): odd +0.8 / +1.3 / +3.3 dB at 1,000 / 3,000 / 6,000 rpm against the even V10's -17.2 / -9.4 / -4.3; sub orders -2.4 / -2.9 / -1.0 against -20.0 / -19.5 / -15.6.

### Supercharger lobes

`blowerLobes` accepts 8 now: a Roots rotor with four lobes (the 2013 GT500's TVS 2300) makes eight passes per rotor turn, twice a twin screw's four. Nothing else changed: the whine's fundamental is rpm / 60 × `blowerRatio` × `blowerLobes`.

## Turbo and accessory behaviours

All of these live in `engineAccessories.ts` as pure code (a boost model, pulse schedules, a latch, frequency and level functions) that `EngineVoice` turns into native nodes and AudioParam updates and the tests run headless. Each is neutral until the car's `voice` block asks for it; the cost of every one is on the main thread, none touches the worklet, and the sim is untouched.

### Sequential twin turbo

`TurboModel` holds the voice's turbo estimates: `speed` (turbine speed 0..1, the whistle's pitch) and `boost` (charge pressure 0..1 after the changeover, the level of the whistle, the whoosh and the valve). Without a sequence both are the one follower of pedal times revs the voice always had (asserted bit for bit against the old formula). With `handoverRpm`:

- **Primary.** The pressure follows `load x clamp((rpm - 1000) / (spoolRpm - 1000))`, a plateau reached at 1,800 rpm and held to the redline (a sequential car is flat), with a quicker spool than a big single (0.25 s against 0.408 s, est.); the whistle's pitch still follows the turbine speed (revs).
- **Pre-boost.** From `preboostRpm` to the handover the second turbine turns on exhaust routed to it but makes no boost, so its tone is a hum at 0.25 of full, a time constant of 0.35 s behind the revs; the primary's whistle is unchanged. The Supra's source calls this "pre-boost, no extra output yet".
- **Changeover.** The first time the revs cross `handoverRpm` with the pedal over 0.3 (it re-arms 250 rpm below, so a rev hovering at the boundary changes over once) the boost follows a timeline: a cosine down by `handoverDip` over 0.12 s, back up over 0.3 s, then a surge of half the dip over the plateau (a half sine, 0.5 s). Meanwhile the second tone joins to full (0.1 s time constant, 0.8 of the primary's level, 0.78 of its pitch since the second turbo is bigger) and the primary's whistle gives way to 0.7 of its level and 0.85 of its pitch as the flow is shared. The dip and the surge scale the whistle, the whoosh and the valve's release, so everything boost drives follows.
- **Sources** (docs/carEngines.md): the 13B-REW makes about 10 psi from about 1,800 rpm, the second turbo prespools near 4,000, the changeover is at about 4,500 with a brief drop to about 8 psi (Wikipedia RX-7); the 2JZ-GTE's first turbo gives its torque at 1,800 rpm, exhaust is routed to the second at 3,500 rpm (pre-boost) and the second joins at 4,000 (Wikipedia Supra); no dip is described for the Supra, so `handoverDip` is 0 there. The shapes and constants (dip timings, the surge, the hum and shares) are our estimate.
- **Measured** (test render, 240 fps model, 1,600 rpm per second through 4,500): the Kazama's boost dips 20 % (0.99 to 0.80, the sourced 10 to 8 psi) 0.12 s after the crossing, is back at the plateau 0.42 s later and surges to 1.09 (half the dip over it); the second tone goes 0 below 3,900 rpm, 0.08 at 4,350, 0.74 at 4,584, 1 above 4,800; the Zenith has no dip and its second tone is 0.02 at 3,400, 0.14 at 3,960, 0.93 at 4,160. The dip is the same at 30, 60 and 120 fps (within 0.04). In a render of the whole voice the Kazama pull is +0.3 dB over the same pull before in every window (the early plateau puts the whistle in sooner), the Zenith's +0.3 to +0.4.
- **Cost:** `TurboModel.step` 57 ns (a handful of exponentials), one more oscillator and gain per sequential voice (not on rivals), two more AudioParam updates a frame; the worklet is unchanged.

### Surge flutter and wastegate chatter

A lift at boost (the pedal was over 0.6, is now under 0.25, boost over 0.35) is one event with several parts, each optional:

- **The valve** as before (`vent`, `recirculating`), or none (`diverter: 'flutter'`).
- **Compressor surge flutter** (`FLUTTER`): the throttle shuts on a spinning compressor with nowhere for the charge to go, the flow reverses and the compressor stalls and recovers repeatedly, each stall a puff. A train of 3 to 9 noise pulses by boost (4 ms attack, each ringing for 0.6 of its period), 14 a second slowing to 6 over a band sweeping 1.5 kHz to 650 Hz, each quieter than the last; the turbine keeps 0.7 of its speed. A valve car flutters only on a hard lift (pedal over 0.85 at boost over 0.6) scaled by `voice.flutter`; a valveless car on every lift. Rates and counts are our estimate (the docs name the flutter as a gap for the RX-7, Supra and MR2; no figure).
- **Wastegate chatter** (`CHATTER`): on a lift from boost over 0.5 the exhaust flow collapses while the gate is just opening and the valve rattles on its seat: 6 to 11 ticks (0.6 ms attack, 3 ms decay, a 2.6 kHz band), 16 to 34 ms apart (grown across the burst, jittered by 25 %), fading to 0.3 of the first, starting 30 ms after the lift; the burst lasts under 0.4 s. Our estimate.
- **Measured** (offline render of the whole voice, an inline six at 6,700 rpm lifting at boost 0.8, accessories alone against the engine in the same 0.3 s window of -20.7 dB): the vent -29.8 dB, the recirculating pssh -38.0, flutter alone -32.2, a recirculating valve with a flutter share of 0.6 -33.9, chatter on top of the valve -37.1 (short ticks: peak 0.18 against the valve's 0.14). Test: the pulse train's envelope has its first gap at 11 to 16 Hz, its last at 5 to 8, strictly slowing and fading, and the chatter's 6 to 11 ticks sit 16 to 34 ms (with jitter) apart.
- **Fleet:** every shipped turbo car now has `diverter: 'recirculating'` (stock cars; nothing in the data says otherwise, and the Kombi already had it), so their lift is the soft pssh and not the vent's chuff, 6 dB lighter. Surge flutter: Tachibana Runabout 0.5 (its section's "a flutter on lift"), Tachibana Zenith 0.4 ("BOV flutter" gap), Kazama Rotor 0.3 ("relief-valve flutter" gap). Wastegate chatter: Tachibana Runabout 0.6 ("wastegate whoosh and chatter"), Hinode Horizon 0.4 (its wastegates are sourced). The Horizon's parallel twin turbos are the one `twinWhistle` car. No stock car runs `diverter: 'flutter'`; it is for a build with a deleted valve.
- **Cost:** a lift schedules about 30 automation events once (an array of 3 to 11 pulses, the only allocation, 0.35 microseconds to compute both); a flutter voice adds a noise source, a band pass and a gain (3 nodes), a chatter voice the same, none of them JavaScript per sample and no AudioParam updates per frame.

### Active exhaust flaps

`flaps` (0 to 1 depth) puts a gain and a low pass in front of the silencer's convolver, shut (quiet, dull) until load or revs open them: the flap latches open at pedal 0.6 or 0.7 of the redline and shut only under pedal 0.35 and 0.6 of the redline (a hysteresis band between them holds the state), and travels with a 0.15 s time constant (`damp`, so 30 and 120 fps travel the same: 0.2 s of floor gives 1 - exp(-0.2 / 0.15) at every rate). Shut at depth 1 is 0.35 gain (-9 dB) and a 1.4 kHz low pass, open is the program's own response, which the loudness trim is measured against; depth scales the difference. Pops are dry and past the flaps. Candidates: TT-RS, Corvette Z51, the 2013 GT500 (docs/carEngines.md); the thresholds are our estimate. No shipped car sets it. Measured on an inline five at `flaps: 1`: cruise (pedal 0.2) -27.2 dB against -17.6 without, floored (4 to 5 s) identical, the flap 0.54 open 0.1 s after the pedal goes down, 0.88 at 0.3 s. Cost: two nodes, two AudioParam updates a frame; the convolver stays one.

### Air-cooled fan whine

`fanRatio` adds the engine-driven cooling fan: a blade pass whine at rpm / 60 x ratio x 11 blades, a second harmonic at 0.4, locked to the crank, always present and rising with revs (0.3 of its level at idle, the rest by (rpm / redline)^1.5), amplitude 0.012 at the redline. The 993's ratio and blade count are not in docs/carEngines.md (it says only "fan whine from the cooling fan (rpm-locked)"), so 1.8 and 11 are estimates, marked est.; at 1.8 the pass is 1.8 kHz at 5,600 rpm. Measured on a flat six with the engine silenced and the whistles muted: 20 dB over the bed at the blade pass at 5,600 rpm (-54.9 dB against -75.6 without), hidden under the engine's own harmonics at 1,800 rpm, and 31, 28 and 25 dB under the engine's RMS at 1,000, 3,300 and 5,600 rpm (-47 / -43 / -40 against -16 / -15 / -15): faint, as asked. Cost: five nodes (two oscillators, their gains and a level) and three AudioParam updates a frame. Suits the 993 (the planned Porsche), nothing else is air cooled. No shipped car sets it.

### Gear whine

`gearWhine` is a gearbox mesh tone at 24 teeth x the gearbox output shaft's revolutions (partials 1, 0.4, 0.15): locked to wheel speed times the final drive, so it is the same pitch at the same road speed in every gear, silent when the car stops (faded in over 150 to 300 rpm of the shaft), cut with the drive during a shift, and 0.45 of its level off the pedal. The level field is a multiple of 0.03 at full load (a dog box at 1): measured on a mid V6 pulling through four gears, the whine is the peak at exactly 24 x shaft rev/s (583, 979, 1,382 and 1,915 Hz found for 583, 971, 1,407 and 1,943 expected within the search step), 7 to 12 dB over the bed where the gears are, and -15 dB against the engine's RMS at full load (solo -30 dB against -15). The tooth count is a plausible figure, not a source.

Input: `EngineInput.driveRpm` (gearbox output rpm = speed / wheel radius x final drive x 30 / pi), computed in `RaceSession`'s engine update from the pose's speed and the car's tuning, read only; the garage's free revving engine passes none and so stays silent. Suits the cars docs/carEngines.md names: the NSX-R (straight cut options), the S2000 and any straight-cut build (the 993 Clubsport's shorter ratios are the other candidate); a stock road gearbox would be around 0.2 to 0.4. No shipped car sets it.

2. **Exhaust modes** per car (a valved silencer: cruise vs sport): active exhaust flaps are done (`flaps`: shut and dull at cruise, open on load or revs, no shipped car yet); choosing the mode in the garage like a part is still to do.

4. **Nitrous** backfire pops through the silencer when the shot ends, instead of the separate hiss only (the overrun pop path can carry it: a bang with fuel 1).
5. **Gear whine** is done (`gearWhine`, locked to the gearbox output shaft by `EngineInput.driveRpm`; no shipped car sets it); the road noise stays a separate SFX.
6. **Phones.** Measure the worklet and four convolvers on a mid Android device; `lite` already halves rivals' response length, and the next knob is one shared convolver for all rivals.

## How to work on it

- Headless: `buildProgram(spec)` and `EngineSynth.render(...)` run under `npx tsx` or Vitest; `tests/engineVoice.test.ts` has `unevenness()` (energy at cycle harmonics off the firing grid vs on it, in dB) and `tone()` (Goertzel at one frequency) to compare programs without listening, and `renderImpulse` gives the silencer response to inspect. Rendering to WAV for listening is a 20 line script (see the session that built this, or write the Float32Array as 16 bit PCM).
- In the browser: `/dev` with `?garage`, press the gas (Space) to rev; the Street view cruises at part throttle. `e2e/engine.spec.ts` checks the worklet registers and a voice runs. Levels were balanced by driving each car's `EngineVoice` from a Playwright script with an `AnalyserNode` on `audioHub().engineBus` (headless Chromium renders the graph): at full throttle the four cars read 0.12 to 0.16 RMS there, idle 0.03 to 0.07, with the bus compressor after it; the share of power above 5 kHz sits 13 to 26 dB under the whole.
- Two things that bit while building it: `ConvolverNode.normalize` (on by default) buries a long response by 25 dB, so it is off and the response is peak normalized instead; and two symmetric tailpipes summed in mono cancel a cross-plane bank's unevenness exactly, hence the stereo pipes and the crossover.
- References to keep handy: engine-sim's `assets/engines/**/*.mr` for firing orders (`ignition_module.connect_wire`), bank angles and primary lengths; its `es/sound-library/impulse_responses.mr` for which responses it pairs with what; the layout tables above for the real cars.
