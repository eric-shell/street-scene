# Street Scene

A browser street racing proof of concept in the spirit of NFSU2. You race at night through a procedurally generated neon city in tuner cars, with deep visual and performance customization in a garage. It runs on desktop and phone.

- Daily seeded city with circuit, sprint and drift events
- Custom deterministic arcade car physics with drifting and nitrous
- Deterministic AI rivals with a personality rolled every race (their own racing lines, corner cutting, shortcuts, racecraft from patient to all elbows) and mild rubber banding
- Garage with bumpers, skirts, spoilers and wheels, paint finishes, vinyls, stance, tint, underglow and a setup (suspension, gear ratio, differential and brake balance sliders), shown on a turntable in a detailing studio or out on the event's streets
- The menu films your own car cruising tonight's event route like a car advert: tracking alongside, chasing, a roadside pass, an orbit, a crane over the street
- Replays and ghosts from input logs, plus a drone flyover mode with its own minimap (also a photo mode in any race: press 0)
- Number key cameras and a mouse drag that orbits the camera around the car at any time
- Synthesized engine sound (a pulse train per cylinder through each car's own headers and silencer: the Wendt Strom is a narrow-angle VR6 on one pipe, the Kazama Rotor a twin turbo twin rotor, the Brandt Kombi a turbo inline five on one system, the Ridgeway Fiend a supercharged V8) and a persistent music player
- Regional architecture: neighbourhoods that blend New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Philadelphia and Miami
- The river is a racing venue: 9 seeds in 10 get the concrete channel, drivable end to end under every bridge (two levels: the deck above, the floor beneath), with 6 to 8 entrances (fords and side ramps, one in every quarter of its length, fenced and lit like the shortcuts) and routes that dive in and race the floor
- About half the city is special districts: two to three hills (domes, ridges and basins up to 16 m with 11% grades), two to three parks, a floodlit monument plaza on a gentle dome, roundabouts whose islands you drive through (five kinds: mound, knoll, bowl, sunken plaza, garden), alleys, the river and the El
- A weather mood per seed (clear, hazy, misty, drizzle or rain) with a news helicopter sweeping a searchlight, a blimp with an LED band, airliners, meteors and a moon with its real phase
- Sparks when a car bottoms out or scrapes a wall or rail
- Street polish: manholes, drains, tar, oil, worn crosswalks, lane arrows, bike lanes, painted curbs and stencils, rubber on the route's corners, puddles, posters, grime, blade signs, banners and steaming manholes
- Destructible city: street furniture plus cafe tables, bushes, planter pots, bollards, crates, barrels, dumpsters, news boxes and trash bags in corner plazas, parks, alleys, roundabout islands and the monument plaza; back alley kicker jumps
- Spectator crowds at the start and finish, and a street meet behind every cyan barrier: a crowd packed against it, then customized cars parked nose to the course
- Every circuit and sprint has at least one construction-site shortcut: a slim cobbled back alley fenced off at both ends that you smash through, and it really pays off
- An elevated railway on most seeds, with trains running both ways and stations
- An elevated expressway across the city on three seeds in five, drivable in free roam: three lanes each way with long median crossovers, sign gantries and lamp posts; interchanges where an off-ramp comes down alongside the deck to slip into the street beneath just before a junction and an on-ramp peels off it just after, on long acceleration and deceleration lanes; no dead ends, both ends curl off into cloverleaf loops down to the street beneath; and alleys under the ramp lanes to cut through
- A named event per seed (Hill Climb, River Run, Harbor Run, Alley Sprint, Under the El, Ring Rush, Grand Tour, Downtown Dash) with routes picked to match
- Timed hazards: a gated level crossing where the El runs at grade over a bluff
- Ambient traffic: civilian cars on the streets the race doesn't use, and on every street in free roam
- A night market of lantern-lit stalls to smash through, multi-storey car park jumps, tunnels under buildings and through ridges and freeway viaducts
- Street variety: bus bays, hydrants that gush, parking meters, mailboxes, phone booths, vending machines and roadworks lane closures
- Drift zones on the monument plaza and parking lots, a police helicopter on the leader in the last lap, and lightning on rain nights
- Free roam: the whole city of the event seed (or a new random one) with no route, rules or rivals
- Debug mode (Settings > Developer): live physics and camera tuning panels with JSON export, in races and free roam

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5175
npm test           # unit, determinism, AI and replay tests
npm run build      # typecheck + production build into dist/
npm run assets     # convert source FBX cars, build parts, validate catalog
npm run validate   # validate the parts catalog only
npm run inspect    # print node/material/triangle info for GLB files
npm run music      # import albums / rebuild the music catalog (see docs/music.md)
```

Useful URL parameters: `?seed=anything` (custom event), `?race=circuit|sprint|drift` (jump into a race), `?freeroam`, `?garage`, `?drone`, `?quality=low|medium|high|auto`, `?fps` (fps, frame time, auto graphics level), `?dev` (garage dev mode), `?build=<code>` (load a shared build).

Drift by tapping the handbrake into a corner (it locks the rear wheels, and on a car that drives them drops the clutch, so the engine revs free until you let go and the wheels light up), or with a clutch kick (lift and stab the throttle while steering hard), then hold it on the throttle with counter steer. The driven wheels have real speed: with **traction control** off (the default, Settings) more throttle than the tires can take spins them up toward the limiter, and a spinning tire trades its sideways grip for the forward bite it is making, so in a slide the throttle sets the angle and lifting hands the grip back. Launches at high revs, low gears mid corner (power oversteer on the rear wheel drive cars, understeer on the all wheel drive, front biased Wendt Strom), sidewalks, dirt, crests and clutch kicks all spin them. Full counter steer lock reaches the direction of travel at any angle; past 55° of slide an aid turns the nose back. Traction control keeps the tires inside their grip peak and cuts power in slides; AI opponents always drive with it. Braking with the handbrake pulled stops the car along its path and never selects reverse: reverse needs a standstill with the brake held. Nitrous refills from drifting, near misses with walls, drafting behind opponents and wrecking street furniture, and a callout over the dials names each one with the points it paid, rolling up as it pays (1,000 is a whole stage), with a rising tick every hundred, a chime and a pad kick every thousand and a fanfare when a stage fills. Points past a full tank banks **overdrive**, a gold strip along the bar's foot (up to a stage of it) that burns alongside the tank and pushes every stage 25% harder. A hard hit on a wall, barrier, rail or another racer costs nitrous, overdrive first (both cars in a crash pay), counted in red. Every point style paid less what crashes cost is the race's **points**, counted up on the results screen with a line per source, kept as a best per event and added to a lifetime total (saved with the account, the source for future unlocks). The tank holds three stages, cyan, purple and pink on the bar, filled in order; a boost burns the top stage first, and each stage pushes a little harder than the one below, with more flame, warp, camera shake and music. Hold the boost from at least one full stage until the tank runs dry and it chains: a third of a stage back for each full stage you started with (a whole stage from a full tank, which can chain once more). In replays, **C** cycles chase, TV, orbit, close, hood and free cameras. Drone mode uses WASD + drag; Space/Ctrl move up and down, Shift boosts, T toggles the auto tour. In a race or free roam, **0** freezes the action and hands the camera to the drone from where it is; 0 or Esc flies back to the car.

The engine voices follow the pulse train model of **Engine Simulator** by Ange Yaghi ([github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim), MIT): each cylinder's exhaust pulse travels its own header to the tailpipe, the sum is jittered, differentiated and noise modulated, then convolved with a silencer response (ours is synthesized rather than recorded). See [docs/engineSound.md](docs/engineSound.md) for the design and the plan for more engines.

**Authored cars** live in `assets/source/eshell/<carId>/<carId>.blend`, modelled directly in car space (meters, Blender Z up, car facing -Y, origin on the ground between the axles) at one shared scale: every car rides on the same wheel (`standardWheel` in `scripts/assetConfig.json`, 0.28 m radius, 0.24 m wide), so cars read at their true size next to each other. Each file has four collections: `Car` (`Body` plus the stock `Bumper_Front`, `Bumper_Rear`, `Skirts`, `Hood`, `Roof` the part slots replace), `Parts` (one object per catalog part, named `<slot>_<partId>`, in garage order, with an optional `displayName` property), `Wheels` (wheels in wheel space, `wheel_<n>`) and `Rig` (empties `Wheel_FL/FR/RL/RR` at the wheel centers). `scripts/blender/exportCar.py` exports it; the car itself is never measured or rescaled. A new authored car needs its manifest entry (display name, engine, paint, `selectable`) before the first build.

**References.** Each authored car is a blend of two real donors (`scripts/blender/carSpecs.json`: sourced dimensions, blend weights, what to take from each, lamp signatures) under one house stylization. `npm run audit -- <carId>` measures the shipped GLB against the blended donors and checks the shared features (pillars, glass, mirrors, handles, cowl, lamps, indicators, interior, arch gaps). Photos and product pages go in `assets/refs/` (`assets/refs/README.md`); in Blender `carRefs.py` places calibrated photos behind the car and renders comparison sheets, and `carFeatures.py` builds every shared feature the same way on every car. The design language and kit sizes are `docs/carDesign.md`; the process is the `/car` skill.

**FBX imports** (the old path, kept for new IceMaan downloads): unzip into `assets/source/icemaan/<folder>/` and list the car under `cars` in `scripts/assetConfig.json` (folder names, target length). They are converted, normalized by measuring the arches and rescaled to the target length; their body kits stay single merged meshes.

Then run `npm run assets`. It:

1. Exports every authored `.blend` with Blender in background mode (`BLENDER` env var, `blender` on PATH, or `/Applications/Blender.app`), only when the file or the exporter changed.
2. Converts each FBX to GLB (Blender, otherwise FBX2glTF from the `fbx2gltf` npm package), normalizes it to car space (front from the headlight/taillight materials, axles by ray casting the wheel arches) and renames source materials to the shared vocabulary (`materialMap`).
3. Writes Meshopt-compressed cars to `public/cars/` and parts (bumpers, skirts, spoilers, universal wheels) to `public/parts/`.
4. Updates `src/assets/carManifest.json` and `src/assets/partsCatalog.json` (geometry and generated entries; hand-written entries and fields stay), writes `assets/inspect.md`, and runs validation. It fails loudly on errors.

**Current status:** the Wendt Strom, Kazama Rotor and Brandt Kombi are stock donor bodies (one `Body` each, no part slots yet: bumpers, hoods, roofs, spoilers and the Kurogane, Hayate and Tsubame kits are to be rebuilt on the new bodies) sharing the set of eight wheels. A car added to the manifest without source files renders as a code-built placeholder (box body, cylinder wheels) behind the same `carView.ts` interface, using the provisional dimensions in the manifest.

## Adding a new part

See [docs/partsAuthoring.md](docs/partsAuthoring.md). In short: model it in car space using the shared material names, export a GLB to `public/parts/<carId>_<slot>_<partId>.glb`, add one entry to `src/assets/partsCatalog.json`, and run `npm run validate`. To try a part before cataloging it, open the garage, press Ctrl+Shift+D, and drag the GLB onto the window.

## Tuning cars

Every driving number lives in one hand editable file, **`src/sim/drivingParams.json`** (`src/sim/tuning.ts` only types, merges and validates it):

- `base`: the tuning every car starts from: a front inline 6, rear wheel drive. The retired Proto Compact drove it; no shipped car drives it as is, and the physics tests drive it through `baseTuning()` (`tests/helpers.ts`).
- `cars.<id>`: per car overrides merged over `base`, written as absolute values (`"mass": 2000`). Nested objects merge key by key; arrays (`torqueCurve`, `ratios`) replace whole. This is also where each car's `drivetrain` (`"rwd"`, `"awd"` or `"fwd"`, plus `awdFrontShare` for AWD) is set.
- `setup`: the garage Tune tab's four sliders, five positions each (3 is the stock car), that `applyBuild()` reads.
- Keys starting with `_` are comments. Unknown keys and wrong types fail loudly at startup. Geometry comes from `src/assets/carManifest.json`.

The **physics tuning panel** shows every driving parameter of the car (drivetrain, engine, torque curve, gear ratios, tires, traction and wheelspin, suspension, brakes, steering, aero, nitrous, drift aids, surfaces, collisions) as a live slider, grouped by section, with a filter box that also searches descriptions. Hover a name for its one line description, or tap it to show the description inline. Changed values are highlighted. Turn on **Debug mode** in Settings > Developer: both tuning panels open in every race and free roam, and **`** / **F2** (physics) and **F3** (camera) hide and show them. Physics tuning is kept per car and re-applied on the next free roam in debug mode. It's discarded automatically when `drivingParams.json` changes, so it never masks edits to the file.

- **Copy JSON** / **Download** export `{ nightRunTuning, carId, performance, changed, fileOverride, tuning }`. `changed` lists only what you moved. `fileOverride` is the same change as a block to paste into `cars.<id>` in `drivingParams.json` (exported only on the stock setup).
- **Import** accepts that same export (it applies the full `tuning`), a `drivingParams.json` style car block, or a flat `"path": value` map.
- **Reset all** returns to the car's stock tuning with its Tune tab setup.

Runs with changed tuning are never saved as best times or ghosts.

### Rules the code follows

1. **The simulation is pure.** `src/sim/` and `src/city/` never touch DOM, `window` or three.js. Given a seed, a performance build and a stream of input frames, they produce the same state. They run headless in Vitest today, and could run on a server to verify leaderboard times later.
2. **Determinism.** No `Math.random`, `Date`, `performance.now` or frame time in the sim. The seeded mulberry32 PRNG is the only randomness. Physics steps at a fixed 60 Hz (two substeps). AI lives inside the sim. Trig used by the sim goes through `src/sim/math.ts` so it can be swapped for a portable implementation.
3. **Rendering only reads state.** `RaceSession` keeps the previous and current snapshots and interpolates with the loop's alpha. Nothing in `src/render/` writes to the sim.
4. **Inputs are data.** Each tick consumes one frame `{ throttle, brake, steer, handbrake, nitrous, shift, reset }`. Frames are quantized and packed to 4 bytes before the sim reads them, so the recorded log replays bit for bit.
5. **Visual and performance builds are separate.** `CarBuild = { visual, performance }`. Only the renderer reads `visual`. The sim reads `performance` through the pure `applyBuild(baseTuning, performance)`. A test confirms visual choices never change the tuning.
6. **All tuning lives in `src/sim/drivingParams.json`.**
7. **Frame-rate targets.** Aim for 120 fps, hold 60, never below 30 (`FRAME_BUDGET` in `src/render/autoQuality.ts`). Physics stays at 60 Hz and the renderer interpolates, so frame rate and tick rate are independent; per-frame work scales with `dt`. Auto graphics buys quality only with headroom at the aim and sells it as soon as 60 slips.

### Tests

- PRNG; city and route determinism; route connectivity, loop closure and unique edges across 200 seeds
- World queries; input packing; physics (suspension, nitrous, handbrake drift, reverse)
- Recorded input log replayed twice gives identical state
- Build validation and clamping; share code round trip; visual choices don't change tuning
- Parts catalog validation against the real files
- Upgrades improve 0 to 100 km/h and top speed, and cover more ground in a race
- AI opponents finish 50 random seeds (circuit and sprint) without getting stuck
- Corner rails are smooth piece chains at the back of the sidewalk, clear of walls and props; dented pieces keep joints under 1/8 car width, steps cost speed, and dents replay identically; free roam's open world has no closures, rails or rivals
- Every closed street is shut by cyan rail pieces (its own chain, or a corner rail across its mouth) that never cross other rails or city walls, head-on closures further back than side ones
- Street meets are deterministic, keep every spectator behind the barrier and off the course, park the cars behind the packed crowd, and thin out from front to back
- Every circuit and sprint over 120 seeds gets an open shortcut that saves distance without skipping too much; a car driving it is credited the stretch it skips, while the same jump elsewhere is capped, and no reset or wrong way fires inside one
- Alley widths, cobbles under every alley road strip and nowhere else, clutter density per alley kind, a clear lane in every alley, and fences at both shortcut mouths that cover every line a car fits on yet break at a crawl
- Railway columns and station stairs are drawn and clear of every road; the timetable is a pure function of time with one direction per track
- The architecture plan is deterministic, mixes all five cities and many styles, clusters into neighbourhoods rather than dicing every building, and never leaves a neon sign hanging above a building
- River: one channel chain from portal to portal, 6 to 8 entrances with one in every quarter, each a ramp edge from a street to a foot on the chain, two rows of fence panels across every entrance; one to three set pieces on the floor (hump, water bar, tabletop, ledge, median) with exact gradients, clear of feet and decks; every bridge is two levels (a car drives the floor under each deck without climbing and the deck without dropping, each with its own walls); a funnel at every ramp top; progress holds on a route over a bridge and through the channel beneath it; the ramp foot has no height step; deterministic
- Hills: two to three per seed in clusters of two to four blocks, grades capped, zero past the slope with C1 gradients, deterministic, clear of the channel, the El, roundabouts and each other; monument walls inside the plaza and off roads, the arch gap drivable; the special district share by street length
- Spark telemetry reports wall scrapes and bottoming out, and scrambling it changes nothing in the physics
- Weather moods follow their weights over 4000 seeds with clear the most common; the helicopter loop is closed, periodic, pure in time and dips over the course; the blimp circle is periodic
- A full race with AI replays identically (finish times and order); solo replays (ghost source) finish with the same time
- Browser tests (`npm run e2e`, Playwright in headless Chromium against the dev server): the boot splash lands the title on the menu and deep links skip it; every session opens on "Astrology" from the top, waiting for the first click when the browser refuses autoplay and playing at once when it allows it

## Decisions made along the way

- **Port 5175.** The dev server is pinned to it (`vite.config.ts`, `strictPort`).
- **Input frame extended.** The spec's five fields gained `shift` (manual gearbox) and `reset` (reset to road) so that both are recorded and replayable.
- **No Blender here, so FBX2glTF.** Normalization happens in Node with glTF Transform, so the Blender and FBX2glTF paths give identical results. The Blender script only converts.
- **One scale, one wheel.** The IceMaan imports were each scaled to a target length, which blew the GC14 up to 4.45 m on 0.33 m wheels next to the S64's 0.27 m. The authored cars share one scale and one wheel size (`standardWheel`, 0.28 m), so the Proto Hatch (now the Wendt Strom) is 4.0 m against the Proto Compact's 3.95 m. It also had its roof chopped 6 cm, sits 5.5 cm lower in its arches and took the Compact's black B-pillars. Both stock cars are plain body colour on the hood and roof; the stripes, carbon and contrast panels are parts.
- **Bodies and bumpers are separate.** Body kit 2 is the base body of both cars, cut into `Body`, `Bumper_Front`, `Bumper_Rear`, `Skirts`, `Hood` and `Roof` along shared planes (the hood and roof follow the panel lines of the old stripe and contrast roof) (ahead of / behind the wheel arches below the light line, and the sill between the arches). Kit 1 and kit 3 first donated their bumpers and skirts, but they looked wrong on the kit 2 body; the aftermarket kits are now built from the stock pieces instead (copy, reshape with displacements that fade to zero at the seam, add splitters, canards, diffuser strakes and blades), so every kit fits exactly like stock. Kurogane is the aggressive one (carbon `Trim` lower apron, deep splitter with end plates, canards, strake diffuser, skirt blades), Hayate the clean one (body colour aprons, an intake bar, slim black lips). Hoods and roofs come from the same tuners: Kurogane carbon with louvred vents and roof vortex generators, Hayate a cowl bulge and a rally roof scoop, Tsubame twin contrast stripes over both. Every option is its own part, so a hood and roof from different tuners mix freely. The `body` slot is kept for future whole-body swaps; the old merged kit 1 and kit 3 bodies are gone, and saved builds using them fall back to the stock body.
- **Buildings are one merged geometry** with world-scale UVs, rather than instanced boxes, so window grids keep their proportions on every building. The street-level podium (2 to 4 floors) uses a separate facade kit atlas with per-level random protrusions.
- **The river is a venue, not a backdrop.** 90% of seeds get the channel; 6 to 8 entrances (fords that dip a crossing street through the channel, side ramps off the bank streets; at least one in every quarter of the length, the outer quarters first, banks alternating) join the channel floor to the road graph, and route finding is biased to dive in, so every river seed's circuit and sprint race the floor. Ramps lengthen rather than steepen: a 36 m minimum run, an eased lip you launch over, a hard kink at the foot (the sparks fire there), and a grade near 14% at most. Entrances are wide and easy to hit: a ford dips the whole road with its plinths on the sidewalks (nothing stands in a lane), a side ramp's trench is 12 m across and both open into a bell at the foot and, for side ramps, a wider one at the street, with chain-link down the plinths and sodium lamps at the foot so every gateway reads from the floor and from the street. The floor drives like road; the water trickle stays slippery and sits to one side. Closures stand fence to fence across the channel and just inside the ramp trenches; there are no corner rails at channel nodes, since the banks and the top fence are the walls. The concrete with its stains and joints, drains, culvert arches, lit portals, chain-link and sodium lamps are merged geometry, five draw calls in all.
- **The channel runs the whole map, under every bridge.** The floor is one chain of channel edges from portal to portal, so a route can dive in anywhere and run its length. The centerline is sampled from the bank rows' own node positions (so it follows a warped grid) and smoothed, with a meander of up to 14 m whose amplitude swells and fades along the way. A bridge is two drivable levels in a 2.5D sim: the terrain stays at deck level (what the road mesh drapes on and what every height query without a car height returns), and the channel profile beneath it is returned for a car body nearer the floor than the deck. The deck's parapets carry a `bottom` the channel cars pass under; the piers on the floor edges and the abutment boxes on the banks are solids with a `top` the deck cars pass over; cars on different levels never touch. Resets never land on a deck footprint. Every ramp top gets the shortcut treatment: fence panels and cones splaying from the plinths onto the street, a sawhorse and a water barrier at each line's end, strings of pink and amber bulbs across the mouth; no sign. The price is route corners: a dive is a long stretch with no street corner to cut, so fewer routes get a second shortcut (130 of 240 against 163) and car parks fit about an eighth of the seeds instead of a quarter.
- **Half the city is special.** Special districts may take up to 45% of the block area: two to three hills, two to three parks, a monument plaza on 60% of seeds and more roundabouts per seed, with alleys, the river and the El on top. Measured by street length, about 42% of streets run through or beside a special district (the old split was about 80/20).
- **Hills.** Clusters of two to four blocks rise on a smooth dome, ridge or basin 8 to 16 m high with a flat top. The flanks hold an 11% grade cap and are C1 everywhere, so streets climb, crest at the plateau edge and drop away; buildings stay (San Francisco styles cluster up there) and everything follows the terrain. Slopes keep clear of the channel, the El, roundabouts and the monument. On a hillside a building sits on its lowest corner and a dark concrete plinth climbs with the ground along its faces, so buried storefronts read as a retaining wall and nothing floats.
- **Monument plaza.** On 60% of seeds a block near the centre becomes an open concrete plaza you can cut across, with a floodlit obelisk, statue column or drive-through triumphal arch on a stepped base; the step ring or the arch piers are the collision, everything else is render only and merges into the district meshes.
- **Roundabout islands are the shortcut, priced in clutter.** No island is a wall any more: every one is driven through, and a city deals its roundabouts five different islands (mound, knoll, bowl, sunken plaza, garden) so none repeats. The sim cannot slow a straight car with terrain (a crest launches it at its speed times the slope and costs almost nothing), so the island's profile is kept near a 6% slope to pitch the car and the time comes from what stands on it: a fence of planter pots and bollards round the middle at a radius no straight line misses twice, lantern posts, benches and hedges to shed speed on, and one solid piece in the middle (trunks, a dry basin, a plinth) to pass at a crawl. Measured headlessly, a clean line through a fenced island at 45 m/s reaches 100 m past the ring in about 6 to 7 s against 9 s for the ring and under 4 s for the old planter; the middle line crashes. The knoll is the one kicker: it clears the ring above about 45 m/s, so lifting is the brake.
- **Regional architecture, render only.** A jittered grid of neighbourhoods (cells about 210 m) blends five cities: New York toward downtown, San Francisco up the hills, Miami along the water, Los Angeles toward the edges, Philadelphia in the ring between. Eleven styles follow from city and height (brownstone, prewar, art deco, glass, Spanish revival, mid-century, Victorian, rowhouse, Second Empire, Miami deco, condo), and terrace blocks share cornice lines. It is decided in `src/render/buildings/architecture.ts` from city data, so replays never change.
- **Facades are weights, not colours.** One 12 layer texture array stores wall, trim and glass weights, so every building gets its own palette in the shader and stays correct at every mip level. Window light is procedural per window: offices light in floor runs, homes window by window, with blinds, slow on and off changes, TV flicker and the odd party window, fading to the average in the distance so towers never sparkle.
- **Massing and details stay merged.** Setback tiers, floodlit crowns and spires, mansards, tile roofs, gables, rooftop water towers, bulkheads, masts with aviation lights, lit billboards, fire escapes, bay windows, sun shades and balconies all go into the existing city meshes; fire escapes, billboards and aviation lights add 3 draw calls. Detail follows the graphics tier. A warm street bounce fading with height, coloured floods and a glass sheen make it readable at night without adding real lights.
- **Collision shapes.** Cars are three circles along their length. Walls are the outer sidewalk line of every road (one sided, which stops tunnelling). Closed side streets are shut by chains of cyan rail pieces, the same heavy modular bodies as the corner rails, so every closure looks and behaves like the rails.
- **Corner guard rails.** Every route corner has a long smooth rail on its outside at the back of the sidewalk (against the building line), so the road and the whole sidewalk are drivable runoff: along the incoming road, around a finely segmented fillet, and along the outgoing road, bending back behind the crosswalks of closed side streets and around street furniture. Rails are chains of 2.5 m modular pieces (`src/sim/rails.ts`), each a very heavy sim body: a hit shoves the piece back by impulse / mass (capped at 0.6 m, a hair of rotation, no spring back) and the dent stays for the race, so a wall rider leaves a stepped rail for the next lap. Neighbors are dragged along so no joint ever opens more than 1/8 of a car width. Low bounce and spin, but scraping drags speed off (`railDrag`, `railSpeedLoss`), shoving a piece costs speed (`railDentLoss`) and scraping into a step costs more (`railStepLoss`). Chevron panels on the rail modules (drawn at their displaced poses) point the way.
- **Cyan barriers and signs.** Every rail piece carries a chevron panel whose chevrons march the way the route goes, an LED strip with light comets running the same way, a soft glow pooled on the ground in front and an amber beacon on every fourth post; shoved pieces are redrawn where they were left. A neon sign floats over every closure and at the apex of every corner rail: a tube frame around big chevrons (along the route past a side closure, toward the turn at a head-on one) that hovers, shimmers, fades in from the distance and fades out again before it would fill the screen.
- **Street meets instead of the yellow barrier.** Behind every cyan barrier with open ground behind it (`src/render/race/meetPlan.ts`) stands a crowd packed against the barrier and thinning with depth, then cars parked nose to the course as if they pulled in to watch: dense in the first rows, where people mix between them, sparser at the back. Each car is a garage model in a stock or body kit variant (spoiler, coloured wheels) with random paint, many with underglow, some front row ones with hazards blinking. The yellow barrier is gone: the closure itself is now the cyan rail, and a second barrier behind the cars would only add draw calls, since a car cannot get through the rail. People and cars are render only and never collide. Crowd density and depth, car rows and draw distance follow the graphics quality, and people beyond 70 m are drawn as a three box stand-in.
- **Light color temperature.** Windows, doors and arches use blackbody colors between 2000 K and 8000 K. Only signs and rooftop trims are neon colored.
- **Inside corners you can crash through.** A share of junction corners (`openCornerChance`) keeps its buildings back, and the infill turns corner voids into drivable pockets: cafe terraces, gardens and market corners full of destructible clutter, shallow verges of bushes and pots where nothing deeper fits. About one corner solution in six stays solid on purpose (raised planters, the odd fountain). A closure barrier that ends where a pocket opens is carried across it, so a pocket is never a way around a closure.
- **Closures stand back from the track.** Head-on closures (straight ahead of the incoming road) stand 7 m further into their street than before, side closures 3 m, so overshooting a corner has room. Where a corner rail already runs across a closed street's mouth, that rail is the closure.
- **Crowd sections.** Besides the start and finish stands, every street meet's crowd is a section (one instanced mesh each, culled by distance). Sections cheer as racers pass and burst into applause; at most eight crowd loops play at once. Both crowd clips are normalized on load to a fixed loudness (with a soft limiter against clipping) and play about three times louder than before, so a meet is heard over the engines.
- **Camera shake and the nitrous warp.** Two shake layers ride on every follow camera: heavy for wall, rail and car crashes and for any sudden loss of speed (more than about 0.45 m/s in one sim tick), light for knocking props over (heavier props knock harder; soft ones like bushes never count as a crash). Each is trauma that decays linearly and shows squared through smooth noise as sway, bob and roll, so it looks the same at any frame rate; the numbers are in the camera panel (F3). Nitrous leaves the middle 50% of the screen untouched; from there to the edges it ramps up a fluid stretch (the picture streams outward through a drifting warp), a zoom blur up to about three times longer, colour fringes, contrast and saturation, and the bloom strengthens. Each nitrous stage runs the warp harder, turns its edge glow from blue through violet to pink, widens the FOV kick a little more and adds a fine buzz to the camera. It follows the speed blur setting.
- **Every route has a shortcut.** Both routes are picked while the city is planned, and each gets one or two back alleys carved for it: a diagonal across the inside block of a sharp turn, a cut across a block the route wraps, or a chord through one block as a fallback. A route with no room for one is swapped for an alternative. A shortcut saves at least 20% of the stretch it skips and never skips more than about a third of the lap; across 1000 seeds most routes get two.
- **Shortcuts pay off.** Race progress credits the whole skipped stretch to a car inside one of the route's shortcuts, while any other big jump is still cut to one tick's driving. Back alleys that are not a real shortcut of the route are closed off like side streets. AI drivers stay on the racing line.
- **Alleys are slim and cobbled.** Raceway alleys are 7.4 m curb to curb with 1.4 m sidewalks, back alleys 4.8 m, route shortcuts 5.2 m, and two to four raceway alleys plus two to five back alleys per seed. Every alley is cobbled between its mouths: a little less grip and more rolling drag than asphalt (tuned in `drivingParams.json`), no lane markings, and a fine camera rumble that grows with speed.
- **Alleys are full of things to hit.** Raceway alleys are lined block after block with restaurant fronts, storage clutter and street works (hoarding, sandbags, water barriers, sawhorses and cones spilling onto the cobbles); back alleys with dumpsters, pallets and crates. Nothing that can stop a car stands within a car's width plus a margin of the centreline.
- **Shortcuts are construction sites.** Each route shortcut is a 100 to 160 m back alley fenced at both mouths with chain-link panels in contractors' colours, plus sawhorses and cones. A panel goes down at any speed and costs a fast car about 2% of its speed. A random back alley that joins two route points gets the same treatment. There is no sign or floor arrow: the fencing, the string lights and the minimap's pink dashes are the tell.
- **Shortcuts bend.** A route shortcut is never a straight shot: its path through the block is a dogleg or a gentle arc (one or two free bend nodes in the road graph, every edge of the chain a back alley flagged shortcut), bent away from the corner it cuts, each bend 16 to 32 degrees and every leg at least 24 m, straight only where the block has no room. Buildings, cobbles, walls, clutter and the string lights follow the bent corridor; race progress credits the whole chain.
- **Jump exits.** About half the shortcuts end in a jump: the last leg meets the route's street at 28 degrees and climbs a parabolic steel ramp (2.2 m over 22 m, steepest at the lip, about 11 degrees) to a lip just inside the alley's walls, through a construction fence, and the car flies over the mouth onto the street heading down it. The drop under the lip is a low concrete face toward the street (`Wall.top`, like the car park's lip) so street cars cannot drive in; the landing zone is planned like the car park's (a run-up speed bound, its flight reach plus a roll-out clear of the next junction, corner rails cut where the flight crosses them, furniture kept off it) and soft clutter lies below the lip and along the landing curb. Measured headlessly, a car over the lip at 60, 80 or 100 km/h lands 17, 24 or 35 m past it on the road and keeps driving; the flight is only 1 to 3 m shorter than the ballistic reach. A jump chain carved for one route is no shortcut for the other route driving it backwards.
- **Elevated railway ("the El").** About three seeds in four get a twin track steel El crossing the whole map along a north-south street, over the curb lane on one side, and once or twice per seed it swings to the neighbouring street through the blocks: an S curve that leaves its street just past a junction, crosses the streets between at a skew (about 40 degrees off square over two blocks, 25 over three) and joins the next one tangent to it, buildings kept out from under it, a pier on each sidewalk of every street it crosses. Riveted plate girders, open ties you see the sky through, a third rail, steel columns with knee braces on concrete footings (the footings are the collision), a truss over the river (always on a bridge street, never a ford's dip), and run-offs past the map edges into tunnel portals in the fog. The bluff fits about half of these lines (it needs five blocks of the El on one street).
- **Trains both ways, from the clock.** Five car trains run a timetable that is a pure function of time, one direction per track: they brake into stations, dwell and leave, so the menu, a race and its replay show the same trains. Medium and High add a light wash through the ties and third rail sparks. A rumble follows the nearest train once the page has had a click or key press.
- **Stations.** One or two per line: an island platform the street side track swings out around, a canopy, lit name boards, a station house under the deck, and enclosed stairs down to the sidewalk with green globes. The stairs collide; the platform and house are overhead. Columns keep clear of where any closure could stand and out of other streets' sidewalks.
- **Street polish is pasted on, never built in.** Manholes, drains, tar, cracks, oil, worn crosswalks, lane arrows, bike lanes, painted curbs and stencils, corner rubber and puddles are quads from one procedural atlas with a matching roughness atlas (tar and oil gloss, paint stays matte): two draw calls for a whole city of them. The rubber follows the route's own corners, braking on the outside of the approach and a fillet through the apex.
- **Walls are dressed by probing what is drawn.** Facades stand an unknown few decimetres behind the collision walls, so posters, grime, pipes, AC units and blade signs find the podium surface by probing its geometry (`src/render/buildings/wallProbe.ts`); low infill walls are exact. Three draw calls: paint (a little self-lit so alleys read), boxes, neon blade signs. A few manholes breathe shader-animated steam on Medium and High, one instanced mesh and one uniform per frame.
- **Pedestrians are bodies, and one plan for everyone.** The start / finish stands, the idle people and the walkers are planned once per world (`src/city/people`) at one density whatever the graphics quality, because the sim collides with them (`src/sim/people.ts`): a tier only sets draw and detail distances. A person in a car's path steps aside with a stateless sidestep the sim and the shaders compute the same way (limited by how far a person can step in the time a car gives them, so a slow car is dodged and one over about 70 km/h is not); a person the car reaches slows it like a prop (momentum exchange with 80 kg, no nitrous, a light camera knock) and ragdolls where the sim hit them, a procedural fall in the vertex shader from the hit tick and fling velocity, lying there for the rest of the race. Bench sitters go down with their bench. New races carry `people: true` in their config; old replays lack it and replay exactly as recorded. The street meets and the market pens stay render only behind their rails. The character itself got a detail pass: faces at the near levels, three wardrobes (race-goers, pedestrians, mechanics), and every lathe turned the right way out.
- **Weather is a mood per seed, render only.** Every seed is a clear, hazy, misty, drizzly or rainy night, drawn to fixed weights with clear the most common (42%). The mood sets fog, sky, stars and moon, lighting and how wet the streets shine; rain is one instanced mesh of short motion-blurred streaks wrapped around the camera that stretch with the car's speed, drizzle only shows where headlights would catch it, and mist is a low additive layer draped over the terrain at High. The sim never sees any of it, so replays stay valid. Low has no rain, splashes or mist.
- **The air is deterministic.** A news helicopter flies a seeded loop that dips over the course, sweeping a searchlight along the road ahead; a blimp with an LED band circles downtown; airliners cross high up and meteors fall now and then. Everything is a pure function of the scene clock and the seed, so a replay shows the same sky. The backdrop's stars, moon, mast beacons, planes and meteors share one instanced sprite mesh, and the sky is three draw calls in all.
- **Tires are a slip vector and driven wheels have speed.** Each tire's force follows its normalized slip vector (slip angle over the peak angle, slip ratio over the peak ratio, the normalization Forza's own telemetry exposes): one grip curve over the vector's length, pointed against it, so a spinning or locked wheel trades sideways grip for the forward or braking force it makes. The driven axles are real rotating pairs (a locked differential) with the engine's inertia geared onto them: under the peak slip ratio they transmit the drive torque at the slip that does so (solved quasi statically, so it is stable at any speed and gear), beyond it they spin up against what the sliding tires give back until the limiter stops the engine, and lifting lets the tires pull them back. That single mechanism is the drift: throttle spins the rear and takes its sideways grip, lift hands it back, the handbrake is a plain rear lock (with the clutch out on a car that drives the rear, so it revs free and lights the wheels on release) and a clutch kick is the flywheel's energy dumped into the wheels. The scripted grip multipliers, the handbrake yaw kick and the velocity carve that used to fake all this are gone; what stays arcade is the 55° anti spin aid, the always on yaw damping, counter steer lock that reaches the direction of travel at any angle, the brake assist (now along the car's path, fading as it goes sideways) and reverse that needs a true standstill. `docs/handling.md` has the model, its numbers and the sources.
- **Sparks from contact telemetry.** The sim records, without ever reading them back, each tick's deepest wall or rail contact (point, normal, sliding speed) and how far any corner pushed past its suspension travel. The renderer turns those into a stream trailing a car that scrapes, a burst on a hard wall hit and a spray off the underbody when it bottoms out. One pooled mesh draws every spark; its flight, drag, gravity, skid along the road and cooling from white to red are computed in the vertex shader, so the CPU only writes new sparks into a ring buffer (160 / 384 / 768 by quality).
- **One minimap for the HUD and the drone.** The drone flyover and the race photo mode show the minimap centred on the drone with its view cone and a north mark, zooming out as it climbs (street detail low, a whole district at the ceiling); in a race it also marks your car and the field.
- **Engine voices by layout.** The car's engine layout (manifest `engine.layout`) picks the voice's character, not just its pitch: a V6 like the Kazama Rotor's is two pulse trains, so it gets a per-bank order at half the firing frequency and a bank to bank beat that growls; an inline six stays silky with a sixth order whine; boxers rasp and burble, cross-plane eights rumble with a heavy beat, rotaries buzz smooth and high. The table is `VOICE_CHARACTER` in `src/audio/engineAudio.ts`.
- **Engine voice for small speakers.** Most engine energy sits below 300 Hz, which laptop speakers barely reproduce, so the voice has a presence peak tracking the firing frequency and runs about 9 dB hotter than before.
- **Design brief per car.** Each garage car is one real donor kept whole rather than a blend of two: Wendt Strom R6, a Golf R32 Mk4 (three doors, front naturally aspirated VR6, AWD biased to the front); Kazama Rotor, a Mazda RX-7 FD (pop-ups modelled raised, front sequential twin turbo twin rotor, RWD, five speeds); Brandt Kombi S, an Audi S2 Avant (roof rails, front turbo inline five, AWD split evenly). The house stylization (roof chop, house lamps and mirrors) is not applied to them, so `npm run audit` lists those rows as off.
- **Reference-driven cars.** Proportions come from two real donors per car blended as ratios to wheelbase, then stylized once in `house` (0.28 m wheel for every car, width x1.06, height x0.92); nothing in the spec is a game number. The audit prints model versus target per station and finds the shared features by material and place, so a modelling session starts and ends with numbers rather than screenshots. The shared features are built by one script from house numbers pinned to each car's own stations (belt line, cabin front, B pillar, bumper seam), which is what keeps pillars, mirrors, handles, cowls and lamp layers consistent across cars. Baseline (2026-10-20): no car had indicators, two lacked an interior, all three noses sat 10 to 23 % short of the estimated donor overhangs.
- **Stock is tame.** Stock bumpers have small grilles and no lips, splitters, strakes or roof spoilers, plain sills and a twin exhaust; the stock hood and roof are body colour. Everything sportier is a part.
- **Spoilers stand on the body.** `mount_on_body` (carKit.py) slides a spoiler along the car until every strut foot lands on paint, lifts it clear of the glass and stretches the feet down onto the surface. Hatch wings go on the roof's trailing edge, coupe and compact wings on the trunk lid.
- **Part names are tuner names.** Aftermarket parts are named after fictional tuners (Kurogane, Hayate; spoilers Tsubasa, Shiden, Raijin; wheels Kumo, Yari, Hari, ...), never Street/Sport/Pro or numbers, so the garage suggests styles rather than a ranking. Only performance upgrades have tiers. A wheel's display name is the `displayName` property of its object in the .blend.
- **Garage cars.** Only the Wendt Strom R6 (`wendtStrom`, front VR6, AWD), Kazama Rotor (`kazamaRotor`, front twin turbo twin rotor, RWD), Brandt Kombi S (`brandtKombi`, front turbo inline five, AWD) Ridgeway Fiend (`ridgewayFiend`, front supercharged V8, RWD; added 2026-10-27 as the Proto Muscle) Hinode Horizon GT (`hinodeHorizon`, front twin turbo inline six, AWD; added 2026-10-28), Tachibana Zenith (`tachibanaZenith`, front twin turbo inline six, RWD; added 2026-10-28) and Tachibana Runabout (`tachibanaRunabout`, mid turbo inline four, RWD; added 2026-10-28) are offered (manifest `selectable`). A build whose car id is not a garage car (a retired car, or one saved under an id from before a rename) loads as the default car with only its wheels kept (`validateBuild`), and a ghost or replay with such a car is dropped (`src/replay/replay.ts`). Vinyls are hidden behind a feature flag (`src/shared/features.ts`).
- **Wheels are the donor's own.** The first wheel set collapsed each donor's face to 1,300 triangles and replaced its lip and barrel with a turned house ring at a guessed radius: 40 fine spokes became mush and spoke tips were cut off, so no wheel looked like its model. The set is now one author's wheels (jonver87; twelve Work at first, 44 from eight makers since 2026-10-27), and the import keeps what the model is: the face at 6,000 triangles with the donor's own shading, the lip and barrel turned from the donor's visible profile (a lathe never crumples, where a collapsed ring did), the bolt circles (half a million triangles on one model) replaced by house hex heads at their own count, radius and depth, and a house tire around the lip. The visible depth from lip edge to face is kept; the barrel behind the face is squeezed to the tire's width and turned all the way back to the tire's rear bead (it was cut off at the face at first, which left a thin disc in a hollow tire: the wheels looked skinny through the spokes and from behind). Multi-piece lips are chrome; a one-piece wheel is one finish, the wheel colour. The garage groups the wheels by their donor's maker under a fictional maker name (Kaito for Work, Mizuchi for Rotiform, Seiryu for Vossen, Byakko, Hotaru, Hiryu, Suzaku and Genbu for HRE, Fifteen52, SSR, BBS and Volk), makers and wheels in alphabetical order so the list suggests no ranking. - **Car spec badges.** Each garage car tile shows its drivetrain code (FR, FA, MR, ...: engine position, then driven wheels) and engine layout (I4, V6, B4, ...), plus a Turbo or S/C tag. The same spec drives the synthesized engine voice, with an optional `voice` block (`headers` with a `headerSpread` in metres, `pipes`, `crossover` for an H- or X-pipe, `muffler`, `intake`, `crank`, and for a supercharged engine `blowerLobes`, `blowerRatio` and `boost` in psi, which set the pitch and loudness of the blower's rpm-locked whine) for the parts of a real car's exhaust and intake that change how it sounds.
- **Engine voices are pulse trains, not oscillators.** Each voice is an AudioWorklet that fires every cylinder in the layout's order and delays its exhaust pulse by its own header length, so a boxer on unequal headers rumbles and a cross-plane V8 burbles for the same reason the real ones do, while inline sixes stay even. The train is jittered, differentiated and noise modulated the way Engine Simulator does it, then convolved with a silencer impulse response that is itself synthesized (quiet, sport or straight through). The intake gets its own output (loud only with the throttle open; throttle bodies honk through a resonance), the valvetrain its tick, and the limiter drops firings at random. Overrun pops are rare on purpose: a hot exhaust (seconds of hard driving) and fuel left by a lift from high revs give a small chance of a bang or a crackle burst, more through a sport or straight silencer, and the same event flashes a flame at that tailpipe. Rivals run a lighter program (shorter silencer response, no intake or valvetrain detail).

- **AI safety net.** If an AI car makes no progress for 4 s, or is stopped for 2.5 s, the race resets it to the road. Finished cars stop colliding so they never block anyone. Down a shortcut or queued behind a moving car, only the stopped timer counts.
- **Every rival drives its own race.** Each race start, retry and restart rolls a new seed (`RaceConfig.aiSeed`, carried by replays) for the rivals' personalities (`src/sim/ai.ts`): aggression and a favoured side spread evenly over the field, then road width used, apex early or late, a corner-by-corner wander, corner cutting, corner grip, braking, consistency, reaction time, nitrous reserve and a taste for shortcuts (about one chance in five per shortcut per lap; a shortcut's detour drives the alley's middle and holds a jump exit's run-up to the speeds its landing was planned for). Each line is a minimum curvature sweep (outside, inside, outside) solved in a frame rounded through the corners, since offsets off the sharp centerline fold over at every node and pinched every car through the same apex. Aggression sets the racecraft: which side to pass (the next corner's inside, or the easy way round), how close to follow, blocking, giving room, and nothing two abreast into a bottleneck; a car alongside where a line turns in is let by, the car behind giving way. - **US units on screen, SI underneath.** Everything the player reads is mph, feet and miles (`src/ui/kit/units.ts`, the one place that converts and rounds); the sim, the city and `drivingParams.json` stay in meters, m/s and km/h, and so do the debug tuning panels, which edit those numbers as written.
- **Cars lie on the ground and land with weight.** The body pitches and rolls within 8.6 degrees of the ground's own angle rather than of level, so a car lies over on a 38 degree river bank or climbs a ramp with all four wheels loaded, and load transfer follows the body's actual acceleration, slope included, so a bank alone never lifts the uphill wheels. The ground pushes back along its normal, so climbing a bank costs speed. In the air the body levels out over the ground below; a landing is soaked up by a stiff, lossy bump stop and rebound damping stronger than bump, so the car squats about 20 cm off the car park jump, rises a few centimetres past ride height and settles in half a second (it used to bounce over a metre). Tire grip stops growing past 2.2 times the static wheel load, so a bump stop hit on one wheel cannot spin the car. Braking is the tires (about 1.1 g) plus an arcade assist of 8 m/s² that leaves cornering grip alone: 160 km/h to a stop in about 52 m, down from 99 m (an earlier 16 m/s² assist stopped in 35 m and felt too grabby). Brake upgrades scale both, and the AI plans its braking zones at 10 m/s².

Hills and the monument are planned after the routes, so for Hill Climb and Grand Tour they come to the race: hills rise on the blocks the routes run along, the plaza sits on a block both routes pass. A seed whose routes can't deliver its event falls back to Downtown Dash, so the name on the menu, intro card and start gantry is always true.
- **Timed hazards run on a pure schedule.** A gate cycle (open, warn, lowering, closed, raising; `src/sim/gates.ts`) is static data and its state is a function of the race tick, so hazards need no state on `RaceState` and replays see the same gates. Gates are one sided walls that block only while closed; the AI brakes and queues for them, traffic stops at them, and the race never counts a car held at one as lost. A car left on the span or the tracks is towed back behind the nearer gate.
- **The channel has things in it.** Every entrance is closed by two rows of fence panels (one across the mouth, one down the lip) that break before they can stop a car, so getting in is a crash, not a turn. Along the floor every seed gets one to three set pieces (`src/city/districts/riverFeatures.ts`): a speed hump, a skewed water bar, a tabletop jump on one half of the floor with the other half left flat, a raised ledge along one half, or a low median with water barriers and sandbags on it splitting the floor in two. They are terrain, not walls (nothing can pin a car and the AI drives over them), with analytic gradients like everything else, 40 m clear of every foot and deck and 90 m from each other, so the closures, meets and the two level bridges are untouched. Clutter doubled along the floor edges and clusters stand out on the floor on the side away from the trickle. The bridges are plain bridges: the drawbridge is gone, the level crossings are the only timed gates, and the follow cameras drive under a deck with the car (the ground and wall tests use the car's level, the deck slab is a ceiling).
- **A bluff along the El.** Where a terrace steps up across the El's column, the line leaves its street for a right of way through the blocks and holds one level: tall columns over the low side, at grade on the bluff top, columns again down the back slope. Streets climb the face on ramps between retaining walls and dip under the El where clearance is short; the street along the top crosses the tracks at a level crossing whose arms come down for the real trains (the timetable moved to `src/city/` so the sim and the renderer share it).
- **Ambient traffic is part of the sim.** Deterministic civilian cars on keep-right lanes with reserved junction turns (`src/sim/traffic.ts`), kept off every route node in a race so a race is bit identical with or without it, and on every street in free roam. Struck cars become loose bodies that slide to a stop and drive on. `RaceConfig.traffic` is off when missing, so old replays play as recorded.
- **Kerb parking is solid.** One kerb of most two-lane streets and boulevards off the route carries a row of parked cars (`src/city/parking.ts`, about 55% of the slots taken, 650 to 900 cars a city). They are traffic cars standing in their slots, not scenery: a car that cuts in hits a real car's mass, shoves it loose and loses the speed, and the struck car skids on its locked wheels, blinks its hazards and goes back to its slot once nobody is looking. The lane on that side moves in clear of the row (one function sizes the strip for both), so no driving car brushes one. They exist in every race and free roam, so replays, ghosts and bests recorded before them no longer match.
- **Night market.** A straight run of route street (one both routes drive, where there is one) is closed for race night: the road narrows to 9 m between lantern-lit stalls, food carts and fruit crates, with a 3.3 m clear lane down the middle so smashing stalls is a choice. The crowd stands in pens behind a red rail sealed at both ends and at the back, so no car can reach them.
- **Multi-storey car park shortcuts.** Where a route turns at a junction with nothing straight behind it, a car park in the block behind lets racers climb a covered ramp into the garage's first parking floor, 6.4 m up over a lit street level, drive through under the levels above and fly out of the facade off a kicker down the street the route turns into (about a tenth of seeds). The garage takes its whole block (the building line inset, up to 30,000 m²; a bigger convex block is cut down behind the drive, an L that big gets none): the block's buildings go, its frontage is the garage's facades with shopfronts in the podium, and the ramp climbs inside, up through a hole in the first floor. Every level is a real parking floor: stalls along the parapets and in double rows with aisles, parked cars in half of them, columns on the bay grid, a stair tower with a lit P, open sides with spandrel bands, fluorescent strips, a roof with lamp posts; the floors are cut out of the block's outline cell by cell. The whole drivable floor is open to the car (progress follows the garage's own line anywhere on it, never lost). It used to be a thin box round the drive alone, with downtown buildings on the rest of the block. The exit lane lies on that street's line between concrete barriers, so a straight launch is never more than 6 degrees off and lands on the road. Parked cars, columns and the stair core on the drivable floor are solid and keep 6 m off the racing line; shortcut bulbs along the ceiling, not signs, show the way, and the apron gets the same funnel as every other shortcut mouth: fence lines and cones on the sidewalk, strings of bulbs across the mouth and the curb, another across the lip. The sim stays 2.5D: one exact height modifier, the levels above are render only (the camera keeps under them, `render/camera/ceilings.ts`), two sided slab walls, and a low wall under the lip that only airborne cars clear (`Wall.top`).
- **Tunnels and overpasses stay 2.5D.** A tunnel is a street with walls (a raised walkway, wingwalls flaring to the building line) and everything overhead is render only, with the camera kept out of roofs and decks. Ridges are the hills profile with the street's corridor masked out. Tunnels and viaducts go on the routes' streets first, never where a race starts or finishes.
- **The expressway is two levels, not a 2.5D overhead.** Like the river's bridges, the world has two drivable levels where its deck runs: a car is on the deck or in the street beneath by its height (`DeckSpan.upper`, World.heightAt), the deck's barriers stop only the cars up there (`Wall.bottom`) and its columns, and anything else standing under the deck, only the cars below (`Wall.under`). Each ramp is a single level up to its last stretch, a walled embankment that becomes a bridge where it leaves its block, at a grade and over vertical curves a car takes on the ground at any speed its turn allows. Interchanges are slip ramps, the way an expressway built over a street meets the street beneath: an off-ramp leaves the deck on a long deceleration lane and comes down alongside it to run along the street at its level just short of a junction, and the on-ramp peels off just past it and climbs alongside to a long acceleration lane, so neither has a turn in it (ramps through the blocks to the parallel street, as they first were, turned square at both ends, and an off-ramp beside each fit on only half the seeds). The deck never ends at a wall: at each end its carriageways curl off on cloverleaf loops of three quarters of a circle down to the street they cross over, which also kept the deck inside the city instead of running on into the fog. The frontage under the ramp lanes is an alley open to the street beneath and the cross streets, where it used to be a walled-off void. The deck is not in the road graph, so races never use it: their ramps are shut at the mouth behind a cyan barrier, and free roam (and the waypoint race's open city) opens them. It runs the El's planner turned on its side (along a row, swinging rows through the blocks) and its deck rises over the El's trains where the two cross. The sign gantries that used to straddle city streets hang over its carriageways now.
- **Street variety.** Some bus stops get a bay (the curb steps back with S tapers; the sim treats the bay as road), hydrants, meters, mailboxes, phone booths and vending machines vary by neighbourhood and broken hydrants gush, and a few streets per city have roadworks: the curb lane closed behind cones and barriers with solid plant behind them. The AI racing line narrows past each closure instead of every piece being soft.
- **Drift zones, the police helicopter and lightning.** The monument plaza and roomy parking lots double drift points and triple the drift nitrous refill, capped per visit (8 s, 0.4 of a nitrous stage) with a 25 s rest, so they are worth a detour but can't be farmed; they're painted like a drift meet, not signposted. On the last lap a police helicopter comes in low and holds its searchlight on the leader. Rain nights get soft lightning flashes through the existing lighting (never a white frame) with delayed, panned thunder; the Flash effects setting turns them off.
- **A setup, not upgrades.** The garage's component upgrades (engine, turbo, exhaust, ... nitrous, each tier strictly better) gave way to four sliders that trade one thing for another: Suspension (Soft to Stiff: springs, travel, turn in, grip on tarmac against grip on dirt and cobbles), Gear ratio (Short to Long: the final drive), Differential (Open to Locked: an open axle's drive dropping to what its lighter wheel holds, a locked one's force pair in a turn; `drivetrain.diffOpen` / `diffLock` in car.ts) and Brake balance (Rear to Front: `brakes.frontBias`). Position 3 on every slider is the stock car bit for bit, so the AI (always stock) and stock replays are untouched; builds and share codes saved with tiers load on the stock setup. The stock cars are drag limited with an overdrive top gear, so the gear ratio's readable effect is the limiter (short gears cap the top speed, long ones let nitrous and slipstreams run on), not 0-100.
- **Pops & bangs is a visual setting** (2026-10-28). The Tune tab's Pops & bangs slider runs 0 to 200 % of the stock backfire rate (100 % is the old look), scaling the engine voice's overrun chances and the shift and crackle flames together so sight and sound agree. It is `visual.popsBangs` (share key `pb`, missing reads 100 %), never `performance`, so the sim, replays and ghosts are untouched.
- **Launch control on the pedals, rolling antilag on a button of its own.** The Tune tab's Launch RPM and Antilag RPM are the build settings the sim reads as is (`performance.launchRpm`, off by default so saved builds, share codes and old replays are untouched; `performance.antilagRpm`, missing keeps the car's own `launch.antilagRpm`): hold the gas through the countdown and a two-step bounces the engine under the launch rpm (a hard cut, `launch.twoStepDepth` / `twoStepCutTime`, which the tach and the engine voice stutter with), so the car leaves the line from exactly those revs. Gas and brake together standing is a line lock (the driven wheels' brakes come off and they spin while the rest hold the car: a burnout, in the countdown too). Rolling antilag is a held button (Shift, left stick click) with the gas, on the move in a turbo car: the clutch slips, the engine is held at the antilag rpm and boost stays pinned, so the release is on full boost at once. It was gas and brake once, but a real ECU's rolling antilag is a button and its own rpm limit, apart from the two-step's (Haltech's Rolling Anti-lag holds a fixed limit while its button is pressed), so each car has a stock Antilag RPM where its turbos are on full boost near its torque peak. To give antilag something to remove, turbo cars (the manifest's aspiration) spool: `launch.turboShare` of their torque needs boost, which builds in `spoolTime` under load and bleeds in `spoolDownTime`. Naturally aspirated cars drive exactly as before. The horn moved from Shift to G to free Shift for it.
- **Nitrous in three stages, Burnout style.** The tank holds three stages of the same capacity (`nitrous.capacity`). Style fills them in order, cyan, purple, then pink on the bar, and a boost burns the top one first at that stage's power (`nitrous.stagePower`: stock, +10%, +20%), so banking style pays without stage 3 driving like another car. A boost held from full stages until the tank runs dry chains a refill (`nitrous.chainRefill`: a third of a stage per full stage it began with), which rewards committing to the whole bar over tapping it. Every source of nitrous pops a callout over the dials with what it paid, in hundredths of a stage so the number matches the bar. Races start on one full stage, as before, and the AI plays by the same rules. The flames, light trails, screen warp and its edge glow, the camera's FOV kick and buzz, pad rumble and a music swell (+2, +4, +6 dB) all step up with the stage burning.
- **Caching (vercel.json).** Vite's `/assets/` files are named by their content, so browsers keep them a year as immutable. Cars, parts, traffic, music, sound and plant keep their names when they change, so they get an hour fresh and a day of stale-while-revalidate: a return visit starts from the cache instead of rechecking every file, at the cost of one load with an old GLB after a deploy that rewrites one. A change that must land at once gets a new file name.
- **A 404 you can drive.** Vercel serves `404.html` (`src/notFound/`) for any path that is not a file: a 2D canvas street in the game's colors with the car to drift around and the way back to the game. Plain canvas, not three.js, so it opens at once; its page view reaches analytics, so broken links show up by their path.
- **The city is chunked, not split.** Every big merged layer stays one mesh with one material, but its index is sorted by 200 m cell when the scene is built and, before each render, its geometry groups are set to the cells the camera can see (`render/cityChunks.ts`), so the GPU skips what the fog hides without a mesh per cell, duplicated vertices or a change to any builder. Three classes: layers that fade into the fog stop at the view depth where FogExp2 has hidden them (depth, not radius, because that is what the fog uses); detail layers (podium trim, markings, wall boxes) stop at a `detailDistance` on the quality ladder; building shells and roofs are only frustum culled, because a skyline silhouette against the sky never fades. In a race about a third of the triangles are drawn for a dozen more draw calls. `?cull=off` in dev builds is the A/B. The same pass thinned what is drawn at all: props by tier, open-backed podium trim, merged wall piers, crowd stands in batches of 48, a light rig per tier, and `npm run perf:scene` plus `?perf=` to measure it.
- **Idle people.** The game's one character also waits at bus stops, sits on benches and at cafe tables, stands under park lamps and chats in pairs outside lit shop fronts (`render/people/idleSpots.ts`, `idlers.ts`): planned from the seed so the menu city and the race agree, never in the curb lane, on the route, by the stands or in a meet, capped per tier with sitters held to half, drawn in 200 m cells at the crowd level up close and the far level beyond, and animated only in the shader (sway, glances, phones, heads turning to passing cars, a flinch from a fast one), so they cost no per-frame loop. A person whose bench or shelter breaks goes with it.
- **Street trees are solid.** Sidewalks on plain streets, avenues, boulevards and diagonals carry about a thousand trees a city (one every 16 m a side, 12 on boulevards, jittered), planted toward the facade with a 0.25 m trunk that is a sim solid like the parks' (`src/city/streetTrees.ts`, `STREET_TREES`). A car that climbs the curb meets the trunk; the canopy overhangs the walk, never the road. They keep 14 m from junctions, 2.5 m from poles and furniture, and off the El, tunnels, the market street, lane closures, bus bays, path mouths, lot openings and the jump exits' and car parks' landing corridors; palms line the Los Angeles and Miami streets. Every drawn trunk is a solid and every solid a tree. This changed every replay, ghost and best time.
- **Plazas are furnished.** Every infill plaza and corner pocket roomy enough gets a centrepiece (the fountain, or a statue on its plinth, a clock column or a kiosk: small solids, wall look `plaza`), solid trees along its back, benches facing the middle, planter rows, bollards a car's width apart along the open frontage, a food cart, news boxes and a ring of lanterns, with festoon strands between the lanterns on the bigger ones; the monument plaza gets a row of trees between its edge lanterns (a car's width off every race pass), festoon and bunting strands, and groups of people; the roundabout plaza gets strands and a group by its basin. A floodlit pool lies under every centrepiece, people sit on the new benches and gather by the centrepieces, and each kind of plaza has its own paving. An alley's mouth pocket takes nothing solid. Sizes and spacings are `PLAZA` in `src/city/config.ts`, the layout is `src/city/districts/plazaDressing.ts`. This changed every replay, ghost and best time.
- **Parking lots.** On most seeds one to three blocks (not the routes', not near a route node or the meets at its closed streets) hold an open-air lot (`src/city/districts/parkingLot.ts`, `PARKING_LOT`): a strip a third to four fifths of the block deep, or the whole block, of flat asphalt with painted bays in double rows round a 6.5 m aisle and a lane all round, a tree island every eleven bays, a ticket booth on an island between an entrance and an exit cut in the curb (the park paths' mouth cuts), more cuts on the other long sides (a corner lot is a way through the corner), lamps, dumpsters and cart corrals at the back, and a fence solid on both sides everywhere else (no gap leads into the block behind). It is an open area, not a route shortcut: the planned routes keep clear of it and no racing line runs through it. The bays hold 45 to 70% parked cars, sim bodies like the kerb's (a hundred or two a lot, seeded kinds with the odd van and pickup, `planParking`) that go loose when hit, and the forecourt behind the entrance is a drift zone. The traffic view draws more parked cars while the camera is at a lot. This changed every replay, ghost and best time.
- **The harbor is open.** A race used to close the quay along its whole frontage with a line of race barriers drawn into the district meshes; the menu's scene, built from the race world, lent that drawing to free roam, whose world had no such walls, so the harbor showed a wall you drove straight through. The barriers are gone and the quay is open ground in every mode, like a park: a dock yard to drive (`src/city/districts/waterfrontYard.ts`). Mouths onto every street that meets it are marked by gate posts with a string of bulbs, lanterns and a band of cobbles, never a sign; chain-link fence runs line the frontage between them. A lane along the water runs end to end with soft clutter in the fast line (pallet stacks, crate piles, oil drum rows, cable spools) against alternating sides, so a clean line weaves past and a car that smashes through pays a few percent a piece and earns nitrous. One or two loading ramps cross it as 6% tabletops (the terrain rule: the hop is free), the deepest stretch is a skid pad drift zone, and working docks get a container lane (two staggered rows of stacked containers, solids) and parked forklifts. Its paving is dock concrete with a grip and drag of its own (`surface.quayGrip`, `quayDrag`). Routes never wrap a block against the coast (none of 89 harbor seeds in 250 has a cut that saves distance without skipping most of the lap), so the quay is registered as open ground rather than a route shortcut (Harbor Run, below, now sends the routes round the quay's recesses, where the lane is one): progress projects a car on the lane onto the street beside it, the minimap and the city map draw its lane in the shortcut ink, meets keep off it, and a jump the route does not know is still held to the distance driven. Measured with a pursuit driver, the lane runs within 0 to 7% of the street beside it; getting on and off through a mouth costs about 1.5 s each way. Replays, ghosts and bests on harbor seeds change.
- **Harbor Run.** The flavour of a harbor seed: the routes run along the quay's street and the block ring behind it, and a lap round a recess of the quay (a warehouse standing where the outer street is missing, a mouth either side) rates highest, because there the lane across is a real cut: the streets go down a block, along and back up, the lane goes straight over. The harbor is planned after the routes and scores a side higher for the races that run along it, so the routes are picked for a preview of the quay (the waterfront's own plan run on a scratch context at route time, `src/city/districts/harborShore.ts`), hills keep their slopes off its frontage so the finished plan lands on the same side, and a seed whose routes miss the quay anyway falls back to Downtown Dash like any flavour (about one harbor seed in twenty). It is weighted to about half the harbors (14, against River Run's 3: only a third of seeds have a harbor, and the quay is that city). Titles: Quay Run, Dockside, Tide Line, Seawall Run, Last Ferry. A route that goes round a recess gets the lane as a quay cut of its own (`Route.quayCuts`, kept apart from its alley shortcuts: no graph edge, nothing to close): race progress follows the lane's own line mapped onto the stretch it skips (`harborCut.ts`, like a car park's ramp; the plain projection looks only two route segments either way, so it never saw the far end of a lap-long detour and credited nothing), a car on it is never lost, and the corner rails at the two mouths come out where the lane starts (they stood across the mouths). Measured with the pursuit driver over a recess (140 to 170 m wide, a 400 to 760 m detour by the streets): the lane takes 15 s to the street's 25 to 36, a saving of 9 to 21 s that is credited in full, no wall, no reset, a few clutter hits; the rivals take it too. A corner next to two mouths on a plain stretch of quay street is not a cut: the lane there runs 3 to 6 s slower than the street, so only recesses are. About 57% of harbors have a recess. Replays, ghosts and bests change on every harbor seed that now draws Harbor Run (the default seed 20261019 among them, no longer a River Run), and on any harbor seed whose routes happen to go round a recess. The finished plan can still pick another side than the preview where hills or the area budget leave it no room; the cost is a fallback, never a wrong name.
- **The SF cable car.** On a San Francisco home (`cityIdentity`, now picked by `src/city/regions.ts homeOf` so the city can read it) one street between two junctions that neither route touches gets a cable car line (`src/city/districts/cableCar.ts`, `CITY.districts.cableCar`): the street that climbs a hill most, else the steepest plain one. A single track down the middle (setts, the slot and two rail heads, render only; the road surface is unchanged), a turntable in the road under each stop and a small waiting shelter on the sidewalk beside it (solids, wall look `cableCar`). The car (`src/sim/cableCar.ts`) is a sim actor whose place is a pure function of the tick, like the El's trains: 20 s at a terminal, then the run at 15 km/h with the grip easing it up and down (`drivingParams.json cableCar`), and back. It is double ended (California Street style) and reverses rather than turning, so its body stays in the track's strip, which every traffic lane clears by construction and kerb parking keeps off. A race car meets it through the traffic contact model at its 7 t: the race car is shoved and stopped, the cable car held to its cable; traffic brakes for it as a foreign body, racers dodge it like traffic. Its bell rings at every departure and at a race car within 25 m ahead (the sim's tick of the last ring is on `RaceState.cableCar`), and the scene swings the bell and strikes it (`sceneVoices.ts CableBell`). The line is on the city map, the minimap and the legend as the "<Stem> Line". This changed every replay, ghost and best time on sf home seeds.
- **Power poles.** The la neighbourhoods (the region cells moved from `architecture.ts` to `src/city/regions.ts`, unchanged, so the facades and the furniture read one map) get wooden power poles (`utilityPole`, `UTILITY_POLES` in `src/city/config.ts`): on an la home every plain street of an la cell, elsewhere a third of the outer ring's la streets, one seeded side, a pole every 35 to 45 m on the outer third of the sidewalk, 14 m from the corners and clear of the lamps, signals, hydrants, bays and furniture (the sidewalk trees, planted after, keep off them). A pole is a 0.2 m prop that snaps like a streetlight (stump, sparks off the insulators, the wires drop to the pavement); three wires sag between consecutive poles of a run, one merged line mesh for the city, and every fourth pole carries a transformer drum. About 250 poles on an la home. This changed every replay, ghost and best time (prop ids after the furniture moved, on every seed with poles).

- **Roads have a crown.** Plain streets, avenues, diagonals and boulevards stand higher at the middle than at the kerbs, a parabola of the cross slope in `drivingParams.json` `road` (1% on streets, 2% on the rest, so a lane's centre sits at about that grade and the kerb side is twice as steep). A car holding a straight line drifts toward the kerb and leans on the crown through a turn; junction fans stay flat (the crown eases in over 14 m past a junction's reach). It is an exact-gradient height modifier in the terrain (`src/city/roadProfile.ts`, layout in `ROAD_PHYSICS`), so `heightAt` and `slopeAt` agree; the road mesh gets quarter-point rows across a crowned street so the crown is drawn as well as driven. Streets a district claims (bridges, the El's street, roundabout arms, the highway's) keep the district's own ground. Lap times over thirty-two autopilot races moved 0.25% (per-race times swing by 2-3% from any change at all: the field is chaotic); every rival still finishes. Like any sim change it changes every existing replay, ghost and best.
- **Speed humps and joints.** On plain side streets no route drives (not the circuit, sprint or drag strip; never avenues or boulevards, never a street a district claims), a 3.6 m long, 8 cm high cosine hump stands every 60 to 120 m (about half the eligible blocks, drawn from a seeded stream), full height across the lanes and eased off toward the kerb so it never ends in a step, with a pair of white chevron bands painted on it. The highway deck and every bridge deck have a 2 cm raised lip every 20 m (bridges: on both abutment lines and between), drawn as a dark strip across the deck; it lifts the deck level only, never the channel floor under a bridge. Neither launches a car (a hump's steepest grade is 7%, a lip is half a metre wide and the springs take it). Each axle that goes over one thumps: the sim records the wheels' fastest compression over a hump or joint (the damper's load spike) and the race emits a `thump` event per axle, which `Sfx` plays as the low suspension knock (loud for a hump, light for a joint, front axle then rear: the "ka-dunk"). All numbers are in `drivingParams.json` `road`. Changes every existing replay, ghost and best.
- **The beach.** A waterfront can be a beach instead of a harbor (`src/city/districts/waterfrontBeach.ts`, `variant: 'beach'`; the renderer is `src/render/districts/waterfrontBeach.ts`). It is rolled from a stream of its own, `beachRolled(seed)`: 80% of waterfronts in a Miami home and 10% elsewhere (the home is `homeOf(seed)` in `src/city/regions.ts`, the same pure pick the renderer's city identity uses, so the city can lean on it without reading the renderer). A rolled beach still has to fit: its coast is pushed 36 m out from the frontage and it takes the longest stretch (at least 360 m, closed by breakwaters unless it keeps a corner) of the free side whose ground stays within 0.8 m of the water's plane at the dune edge, since the hills along a coast run several metres and sand only blends so much; with no such stretch, or a warehouse in the frontage, the waterfront is the harbor it would have been, and a seed that does not roll one plans exactly as before. About half the Miami waterfronts end up beaches, about one in ten elsewhere. From the frontage seaward: a boardwalk (surface `boardwalk`, planks that drive as cobbles) with the mouths and picket fences, palms (solid trunks) at its sea edge with string lights hung palm to palm, lanterns, benches and the pier's root; the dune edge 24 m from the water; sand (surface `sand`, `surface.sandGrip` 0.55 and `sandDrag` 560 N in the tuning panel: from rest it gets a car to 9 m/s in 4 s against 20 on the planks, and sliding it loses speed like dirt but never strands it) draped on terrain rising 2.5% from the water's surface and shelving into the sea, with a surf wall (look `surf`, foam drawn on it) a few decimetres up from the waterline, so no car reaches the water and no sea wall stands where the sand meets it. On the sand: umbrellas with loungers, volleyball nets (destructible panels over painted courts), lifeguard towers (solids, look `lifeguard`), one to two dune jumps (ridges across the dune edge, 0.5 m high with 6% slopes, the terrain rule: a car leaving a crest is launched at its speed times the slope and the landing is the risk), and a drift strip on the hard-packed sand near the water (drift zone kind `beach`, 70 to 110 m long). The pier is a solid from the boardwalk to the waterline and, drawn only, on over the water on piles with lamps whose reflections lie on the water. The surf is animated foam (swash, inner and outer break, scrolling with the wind's gust) and gulls perch on the pier and towers and wheel over the water. At night the sand gets a faint cool sheen of moonlight (an additive sheet, High only), the foam's second and third lines come in at Medium and High, and the whole beach costs about 50k merged triangles at Medium (palms 14k, sand and planks 13k, solids 10k, glow 7k) plus 2k for the foam, in no extra draw call but the foam's one. Like the dock yard it is open ground in every mode: progress projects a car on the boardwalk onto the street beside it, the minimap and the city map draw the boardwalk as a through way, meets keep off it. Replays, ghosts and bests on beach seeds change (and on no others).

## Out of scope for now (but designed for)

- **Leaderboard.** The client would upload seed, mode, performance build and input log (`ReplayData`), and a server function reruns the pure sim to verify the time.
- **Career, currency and unlocks.** The setup tables are already data driven.
