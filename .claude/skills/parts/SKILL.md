# Body panels: stock pieces and parts

What a part is in this game: the base GLB (`public/cars/<carId>.glb`) carries `Body` plus the stock pieces a slot can replace, one node each; a part (`public/parts/<carId>_<slot>_<partId>.glb`) hides its stock node and draws itself in its place. The garage lists the stock node as **Stock** and every part by its `displayName`. So "Stock" is never a part file: it is the piece left in the `Car` collection, and the very first job on any car is cutting it free of the body. Contract and vocabulary: `docs/partsAuthoring.md`, `scripts/blender/exportCar.py`, `src/shared/parts.ts`. Blender mechanics and traps: `/blender`. Kits from product references: `/car` and `docs/carDesign.md` section 4.

## 1. Baseline for an imported car

Every donor import (`docs/carDonors.md`) arrives as one `Body`. Before any aftermarket part, the car gets its baseline split, in this order, each piece checked before the next:

| Piece | Where it parts from the body | Stays with the body |
|---|---|---|
| `Bumper_Front` | the car's own shut line under the grille and lamps, round the corner, along the side to the front arch | lamps and their covers, the wheel well's front wall (`Black`, normal +y) and liners |
| `Bumper_Rear` | the shut line under the tail lamps, round to the rear arch | tail lamps, exhaust tips (`Chrome`), the well's rear wall |
| `Skirts` | the sill between the arches, outside the floor, below the door's lower edge | the doors, the floor |
| `Fenders` | both front fenders: the hood's shut line on top, the bumper's cut in front, the door's front shut line behind, the sill crease below the arch's back edge (optional: only when a car will get fender parts) | the headlamps, the wheel well (`Black`), the A-pillar and cowl |
| `Hood`, `Roof` | panel lines (optional: only when a car will get hood or roof parts). A `Roof` also gets the Paint tab's Roof section (a contrast roof paints its Paint faces), so cut only the panel: the roof's ditches, not the pillars | cowl, glass, headliner |

- **Cut where the car is already cut.** A donor body models its panel gaps as grooves; find them with `groove_z(body, (x, y), direction, z0, z1)` at several stations and read the seam off the numbers (front: rays along +y at |x| 0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.65; corner: radial rays round the bumper's corner; side: rays along -x at several y). Groove depth 3 to 40 mm is a shut line, under 2 mm a crease. Never cut at a round height picked from a picture.
- **Bisect first, then move faces.** `cut(body, planes)` with each plane limited by a `within` predicate to its band (a plane is infinite), then `split_off(body, '<carId>.Bumper_Front', pred)`: below the seam for that station, ahead of the arch opening (measured per height with rays), plus material exceptions for what stays.
- **Keep the shading.** Donor bodies carry the source's normals (`authored_normals`); any bmesh edit drops them, and the exporter would then reshade from the reduced geometry and show every collapse sliver. `split_off`, `bumper_lip` and `patch_out` carry them; any other edit goes between `stash_normals(o)` and `restore_normals(o)`.
- **Budgets follow the donor.** A whole donor bumper is 3,000 to 3,500 triangles and a part adds lamps and a lip to it; raise `TRI_BUDGETS` (`src/shared/parts.ts`) and the table in `docs/partsAuthoring.md` rather than decimate a stock piece (it would shade differently from the body beside it).
- **Record the seam** in section 6 as soon as it is cut.

## 2. References before modelling

Every miss on the Wendt Strom's parts was a misread reference, not a modelling fault: the Cupra R Lip took five builds, the fog lamps three. So:

1. **Read every photo the user gives or links, all of them, before the first Blender call.** A linked page: `WebFetch` lists its images (often as relative paths); download them all with `curl` into `<scratchpad>/<part>/` (an Office-saved page keeps them in `<Page>_files/imageNNN.jpg`) and `Read` each one. Never build from the page's text or the first two photos.
2. **Know what each kind of photo is good for.**
   - Install sequences show how a part mounts: tabs, posts, what is cut or trimmed, where it ends. "Cut the two centre posts in half" meant the lip hangs clear of the bumper on posts in the middle.
   - Product shots show shape in plan, but in perspective. Ends that rise in a front-on shot are the part wrapping back along the sides. A section that looks wider may be a drop, not a bulge.
   - On-car photos show how it sits, and they are the ground truth. When there is none, ask for one before building.
3. **Write the reading down in the reply before modelling**, in five lines: how it attaches, its shape in plan, its section (thickness, how far proud, any gap), where it ends, finish and material. A misread then costs the user one sentence, not a build.
4. **Measure proportions, not sizes**: span of each section as a share of the whole, depth ratios, a lamp's size against its opening, its place as a fraction across it. Photos overstate small round things: the fog lamps came out 20 % big (start a lamp at 60 % of its opening's height, 30 % in from the outer edge, a little above centre).

## 3. A part from a stock piece

1. `copy_part(<nearest piece>, '<carId>.<slot>_<partId>', '<carId>:Parts')`, then `o['displayName'] = '<Name>'`. Copy the nearest part, not stock, when the new one is a variant (Euro w/ Fogs was the Euro plus lamps, the Cupra R Lip the Euro w/ Fogs plus a lip). Names: what the user calls it, else a fictional tuner name or a plain description (memory: part names).
2. **Build an add-on alone first**, in its own collection (never `preview`: `preview()` empties it), and `look` at it from the reference photo's own angle before it goes on the part. Then build it into the copy.
3. **Measure before looking.** Print a table per station (thickness, how far proud, gap to the part above it) and cast rays up from the add-on's top to the part. A render at game scale hid a 3 to 8 cm float under the bumper.
4. **The helpers** (`scripts/blender/carKit.py`, docstrings say why):
   - a removed feature is pressed flush: `patch_out(o, lo, hi, flush=True, ring=0.05, pin=True)` (carDonors.py) between `stash_normals` and `restore_normals`, then `blend_patch_normals(o, lo, hi, ring)`: the fitted normals read a shade lighter than the authored ones round them. `pin=True` moves only the vertices inside the box; on a reduced donor a face centred on the feature can reach far past it, and unpinned the fit drags that far edge with it (Shaved fenders: 1.1 mm at the door's shut line). Then compare height profiles across the box against Stock: outside it they should match to a fraction of a millimetre.
   - an opening closed with new skin (a sunroof delete, a shaved fuel door): `close_opening(o, cut, ring, step, snap, back)` (carDonors.py). Use it over `patch_out` when the feature sits in a gap open behind it: pressing the Wendt Strom's sunroof flat would have left its glass gap as a slot round the panel. It deletes the cut faces, fills the loop they leave with a grid fitted to the panel round it and to the feature's own flush top (`fit_cut`; a cubic, `degree=3`), and `back=('Black', depth)` puts a facing-in copy under the fill so a headliner's hole does not show the paint's back face (Paint is double sided in the game). Judge it by height profiles against Stock along a few lines across the opening, then a top view.
   - a round lamp set into an opening: `round_lamp(o, center, normal, r_lens, r_bezel, proud, segments)`, centre from a ray onto the panel behind it. Lit lamps are `HeadLight`, `TailLight` or `BrakeLight`, the only materials that glow.
   - a chin lip along the bumper's bottom edge: `bumper_lip(src, target, ...)`. Its defaults are the shipped Cupra R Lip (flat bottom, centre plate on posts, full depth outboard, chamfer, run out at the wheel wells), so a new lip is a change of parameters. Underneath it are `lower_edge` (the face's bottom edge round the nose, by plan rays) and `sweep` (a section run along a path with its own normals).
   - a flat splitter cut from sheet stock (HDPE, aluminium): `sheet_lip(src, target, angles, thick, proud, chamfer)`. Flat top and bottom at the nose's underside height; the outline is one sweep through `SHEET_ANGLES` (dense round the corners), smoothed in plan so the donor's noisy edge does not wobble it. `crisp=True` gives straight runs with hard joints, which the user found jagged. It runs to the wheel well's edge by default (`full`, `to_well`: the run before the end is carried straight on to the bumper's rearmost low point, so the ends sit flush with the arch without turning; stopping at a station angle left a 2 to 4 cm gap, a station on the arch's lip flared the ends out, and carrying the end along the sheet's own last direction still bent them 3 to 5 degrees): where the bumper's bottom dips below the sheet it butts against the face; `full=False` stops it short. **`proud` is measured from the bumper's face**, not its bottom edge (`from_face`): a bumper's face overhangs its bottom edge (the Wendt Strom's by 5 cm across the nose, 3 cm on the sides), so a sheet 5 cm past the bottom edge sat flush with the face and the user asked for more. The same holds for any add-on judged from the front: measure what the eye compares it with. Check smoothness by the turn between successive front-edge segments (6.9 degrees at most on Track Mild).
   - canards (dive planes): `canard(src, target, side, a0, a1, z0, rise, width, thick, chamfer, curve, corner, lip, stations)` (`lip` adds a vertical end plate along the outer edge; it stops where a rounded `corner` begins, since round the radius it read as a fold and the user had it removed), a plate following the corner face between two plan angles, angled up toward the rear (`curve` sweeps it up toward the tail), a 45 degree chamfer on its top outer edge, its two outer corners rounded in plan (`corner`, front and back radii; give it stations crowded at the ends, e.g. `(0, 0.04, 0.09, 0.35, 0.65, 0.91, 0.96, 1)`). Measure the root's length off the sections instead of guessing from the angles: the first Track canards were 22 cm, not the 15 cm reported. Where the user steered them: thicker (8 mm), standing out less (5 cm tapering to 2.2), steeper (17 degrees), shorter (11 degrees of the corner, 16 cm), chamfered (4 mm), back on the side face toward the wheel (69 to 80 degrees, ending 10 cm ahead of the arch), curved up (1.2 cm more rise at the tail), corners rounded (1.2 and 0.9 cm). Keep them clear of the outer intake's rim.
   - **A sheet lowered below the bumper leaves a gap** the height of the drop, seen from the side (Track Mild after its 8 mm drop showed 8 to 11 mm round the corners). Close it with `dam` and `dam_face=False`: a thin black filler at the bumper's bottom edge, hidden under the face. Measure the gap before shipping: rays up from the sheet's top to the bumper.
   - bolt-on over-fender flares: `over_fender(skin_objs, target, (y, z) wheel centre, side, toward_end, a0, a1, width, proud, proud_end, lean, bolts)` (carKit.py), one call per arch between `stash_normals` and `restore_normals`. The defaults are the shipped Voomeran (the lip straight out, the face parallel to the panel, a sloped shelf back to the body with the heads on it; the stand-out and the shelf tapering to the legs). It measures the arch itself (rays per station: the first Paint hit outboard of `x_min`, so the well's black lip is skipped; the edge smoothed over three stations) and sweeps a closed seven point section (the lip's foot on the arch edge, its top, the nose, the face's top edge, the shelf's edge on the panel, the step down, the underside along the panel) with `tube` button heads on the shelf; `width_end`, `proud_end` and `shelf_end` taper the band, the stand-out and the shelf toward the legs. Pass every piece the band may lie on (body, fenders, both bumpers): the legs run over the bumpers' arch skin.
   - a shut line across a built piece where it crosses a body seam (a flare over a bumper's joint): `panel_groove(o, only, co, no, half, depth, reach, radius)` (carKit.py), between `stash_normals` and `restore_normals`. Tag the built faces first with an integer face attribute (`only`) and remove it after: the stock panel the part was copied from can end on the same seam (the Wendt Strom's fender edge runs along the front bumper's), and a position test sank it too. Read the seam off the stock piece's boundary edges near the crossing (level, radial, or neither) and give the plane that line plus the direction straight out of the panel; `radius` keeps it to one crossing, since the front seam's level plane also crossed the flare's other leg.
   - side skirt lips: `side_lip(body, target, y0, y1, top, proud, dam)` carries a front splitter's sheet line down both sills between the wheels (same height and thickness, so the line runs on round the car). An add-on part like this needs no stock `Skirts` cut: give the object an empty `hides` property (`o['hides'] = ''`) so the body's own sills stay as Stock, and keep it a new mesh holding only the lip (about 200 to 300 triangles instead of a sill copy).
   - an air dam's top round a bumper's corners (`sheet_lip` with `dam_face`): the line is the ledge or, past `dam_side_from` degrees, the side intake's black surround when it lies within 3.5 cm above the ledge; the line is taken as the highest within two stations there; and the panel stands 3 mm ahead of the face's most forward point below its top, measured from the bumper's own edge. These corner rules apply past `dam_side_from` only: across the nose they made the wrap under the grille worse, so the nose keeps the first rules (its smoothed ledge line, the panel 1 mm proud measured from the sheet's edge). Each was a ragged band of paint between the dam and the line before (check with a material ray map along radial rays, not `look`).
   - slots and a trim plate: `punch_holes(..., stretch=, along='x')` merges neighbouring holes into a slot (`along='z'` an upright oval); `trim_plate(surface, target, holes, cells, side)` lays a plate over a row of them (`side=1` on a rear face), one convex cell per hole (straight dividers, arcs at the ends for a rounded rectangle), each face running from the hole's outline to its cell's edge. A free fill (`tessellate_polygon`) made slivers that sank behind the curved panel, and subdividing them ran to 30,000 triangles.
   - closing a lip's open end at a wheel well: `end_cap(surface, target, y, x_out, bot, ztop, toward)`, a flat panel in the well's plane closing only the lip's own section: `width` from the lip's outer edge to the back of its air dam (section points 5 and 6), from the lip's bottom up to where the dam meets the body. 15 cm inboard, the first build, stuck out under the car into the wheel well. On Track Wild's splitter ends and skirt ends, both the same way: from the back of the lip's air dam (section point 6) 15 cm into the car under the body (the user: "into the car, not away from it"; closing only the lip's own section outside it was the wrong side).
   - splitter spats: `spat(target, front, rear, base, height, rake, curl, curl_height)`, a flat parallelogram plate on the splitter's outer end with its bottom curling outward into a lip. (`end_plate`, a plate along the splitter's curve with its top curled, was the wrong reading of a time attack photo; kept for fences that follow an edge.) Aero reason: they keep the high pressure on the splitter's top from spilling round its ends. A tow strap is `drape_strap(surface, target, x, (y, z), length)`: a flat loop that moves forward just enough to clear whatever is below its bolt (pass the part as built so far, so rims, dams and splitters are draped over); `tow_strap` is the stiff metal loop. `Strap` (red webbing) is the one coloured non-lamp material, added for it.
   - a race splitter: `sheet_lip(..., drop, dam)` lowers the sheet and closes the gap with an air dam in the same L section; support rods are `tube`s in `Chrome`; a tow hook is `tow_strap(target, root, direction, length)` (no coloured material exists: a red or olive strap needs a new entry in `MATERIAL_NAMES` and `carMaterials.ts`).
   - `sweep` orients faces by the section's winding, so L and lipped sections work; the older centroid rule had flipped corner normals at the thin tips of the shipped Cupra lips (left as shipped, 4 corners each).
   - real holes through a panel (speed holes, vents): `punch_holes(o, [(center, normal)], r, depth, sides)`, centres from rays onto the face. It cuts a polygon into the mesh, deletes the inside, runs a black wall `depth` m in and caps it black, then merges the flat slivers the cuts leave and prints any open edge it left. A black disc on the paint reads as a sticker from the garage's three-quarter. 16 sides; check every hole with `ray_map` (a half-covered hole shows as paint inside the `#` circle).
   - reshaping: `displace` pinned to the seam (`plane_seam`); flat plates and fins: `outline`, `slab`, `fin`.
   - a GT wing: `gt_wing(o, span, chord, (y, z) nose, aoa, mat)` sweeps an inverted flat top airfoil (`airfoil`: the NACA thickness law doubled onto the lower side, 15 % thick at 30 % chord) across x with square ends; its uprights are flat plates cut by `cut_plate(o, x, thick, outline, holes, mat)` (a (y, z) loop with windows, both faces tessellated, walls round every loop), each window a convex region between the bars shrunk by `inset_polygon(region, bar / 2)` (a half plane clip: convex regions only, and a region thinner than the bars comes back empty and stays solid). Spoilers have no stock piece (`hides` is empty) and no mount: cast rays onto the hatch for the feet and keep the plates clear of the glass by measured gaps.
   - roof wind splits (fins in the drip rails): `ditch_fin(surfaces, target, y0, y1, x_guess, heights, ramp)` (carKit.py) reads the ditch per station by downward rays (its black bottom's run gives centre and width, the paint either side the lip), smooths it along the car, and sweeps a foot filling the ditch plus a tapered blade with a rounded top, its front rising over `ramp`, both sides. `x_guess(y)` only has to land within 2 cm of the ditch.
   - bolts: `fastener(o, center, normal, washer_r, head_r, washer_t)` (carKit.py) is the house bolt, a 10-sided flat washer under a 12-sided `dome_head` (the user asked that every part use the same rounded washer and bolt): Track Wild's 28 mm rod washers and domed heads, scaled with the plate (Track Mild's tabs 13 mm washers and 9 mm heads, Track Wild's hatch plates 16 and 11). Never a bare `tube` stud. A mounting plate on a curved panel is `surface_plate(o, trees, origin, u, v, direction, size, thick)`: a grid cast onto the panel, offset along the hits' mean normal (a `cut_plate` extruded across x floats on one side of a yawed hatch face); `corner` rounds it in plan (the user: square plates read unfinished; 8 mm on Track Mild's 4 cm tabs, 10 mm on Track Wild's 7 cm plates), and two cells across with three rows per corner arc keep a plate at 80 to 150 triangles (a 4 x 8 grid with arcs ran Track Wild past its budget).
   - **materials cannot be judged by `look`**: it draws every material in one colour and lens covers opaque. `ray_map([objs], x0, x1, z0, z1)` prints what the eye sees through the covers, one letter per material, `O` for a hole into the car.
5. Print the triangle count and materials. A part that lost a slot (the markers' `Light`) is right; one that gained `Paint.001` is not (`remap_materials`).

## 4. Reading the user's feedback

- **"Slightly"** is about 10 % in size, or 1 to 2 cm in position. Change only what was named, leave the rest exactly as it was, and say which numbers moved: "bezel 84 → 76 mm, raised 1 cm".
- **Words for a lip or blade.** Thickness is top to bottom. *Proud* is how far it stands ahead of the bumper's edge. *Taper* means it runs down along its length. A *flat bottom* is one height all the way round. *Chamfer* means a 45 degree bevel; the default is the top front edge, so offer the other edge in one line.
- When the user says it is not close, stop tweaking parameters. Go back to section 2, say what was misread, and build the add-on alone again.

## 5. Save, export, verify

1. `save_working_copy([cid], '<scratch>/work.blend')` (it removes `preview`, remaps materials, unhides the car pieces, saves a copy), then headless `blender -b work.blend --python scripts/blender/saveCar.py -- <carId> assets/source/eshell/<carId>/<carId>.blend`.
2. `npm run assets` and read its last lines (`validation passed`, the size warning). It is allowed in `.claude/settings.json`; run it every round without asking (git holds what it rewrites). Check the catalog entry it wrote (`displayName`, `hides`, `materialSlots`, `tris`).
3. `/dev` garage, Body tab: Stock and the part, switched a few times from the garage's camera, when the user asks or a seam is in doubt.
4. Commit each round the user accepts, and push (memory: push on wrap-up). Stage only the `.blend`, the part GLB, the car GLB when the body changed, the catalog, `assets/inspect.md`, and any budget or doc change. Never stage `public/cars/daltonFiend.glb`: `npm run assets` rewrites it without changing it.

**Order: cheapest to most expensive.** The garage lists a car's parts in the order of its `Parts` collection, and the user wants every body slot sorted from the cheapest real-world part to the most expensive, so the order implies perceived value (Stock, then Euro, Speedholes, Cupra R Lip, Track Base, Track Mild, Track Wild; the Track parts last is a guess, since a DIY sheet splitter can cost about what the Cupra lip does). Place each new part by its rough real price; ask when two are close. Move an object to the end by unlinking and relinking it in the collection, and print `[o.get('displayName') for o in collection.objects]` before saving. An option that fits every part of a slot (fog lamps on any front bumper, shaved markers on any fender) is not a part but a body feature (`BODY_FEATURES`, src/shared/carBuild.ts): car nodes `<feature>_on` / `_off` the garage switches under that slot (`SLOT_FEATURES` in garageTabs.ts), a box for it in the manifest's `bodyFeatures`, and one price for every feature (`ECONOMY.features`, deliberately dear: the user wants all toggles expensive). An add-on is an `_on` node alone; a change to the skin is a swap patch (`_off` the stock faces, `_on` the changed ones, both cut out of the stock piece and of every part that carries those faces). Retired paired parts migrate through `RETIRED_PARTS` (carBuild.ts).

## 7. Keep this skill honest

This file is the memory of the parts process. After every part job, before the commit:

- **Lessons** go in the list below, one line each, dated, with the car: what read wrong, what fixed it, which helper now does it. When a lesson changes the process, move it up into sections 1 to 5 and delete the line. A shipped part gets its row in section 6.
- **Helpers, not inline code.** Anything typed twice in `execute_blender_code` becomes a function in `carKit.py` (geometry) or `carDonors.py` (donor shading, presses) with a docstring that says why. The MCP cannot `inspect.getsource` an exec'd helper, so the docstring and this file are the only place its intent lives. Prove a helper that replaces inline work by rebuilding the shipped part with it and comparing vertices (`bumper_lip` matched the Cupra R Lip exactly).
- **The pipeline docs** change when the contract does: `docs/partsAuthoring.md` for slots, budgets, naming and catalog fields; `exportCar.py`'s docstring for the `.blend` layout; `docs/carDonors.md` when a split or a fix belongs in a donor recipe (the grille back panel that drew a black crescent in the headlamps is there).
- **Open questions** stay listed until answered; answer them in place and say where the answer went.
